## Defining game rituals

[0:01:10] A ritual is a repeatable set of actions that cause some effect within the game world indirectly and the indirectly is important because you might say if I hit a goblin with my sword five times it dies, is that the ritual of goblin slaying? No. For you it can be, sure. But for the purposes of this talk let's say no. Really what we are looking at is you do the series of actions and some external force or system is what will drive the effect you want to happen to actually occur. This is something I looked into last year during Proc Jam.

## Procedural generation

[0:02:00] I made a system for generating some rituals just to create endless spellbooks so they have proc gen contents, proc gen icons and proc gen text. I'll read one of these because the font is a bit flowery. So I have "A CHARM TO BRING FORTUNE TO YOUR ALLY." It says to shatter a pearl upon the earth, and then shout the name of your ally and look toward the west and chant the holy verse and burn a stick of thyme and draw the Bet rune in the air to complete the ritual. The idea is you would be able to do this within a game and it would bring fortune to your ally, for whatever that might mean. Maybe it gives a Bless buff or something like that. I have one to bless a hammer which is a similar kind of almost hokey-poky esque system for doing a particular set of steps to bring about a certain effect.

## Avoiding menu reliance

[0:04:22] In game terms, I am looking at a system for building these rituals procedurally and interpreting them so they can be actually used within a game. An important thing I wanted to have here is it is not just accessed through a menu. So you're not going into your 'rituals' menu and hitting Do Bless Ritual, because at that point it is no different than any other spell system in a game. I wanted to cut out all the UI interactions you would have to directly cast this. Even if you are doing a Bless ritual, you would not get a pop up UI saying "who do you want to cast this on?" I think it's important that information is contained within the ritual itself. That is one of the challenges when you are generating a set of steps to do these things, you need to be aware of what are the requirements that this effect has that I need to satisfy

[0:05:26] through doing the ritual. So, if the spell needs a target, something that I am doing in this ritual has to say who the target is. If I am blessing you, you know, against certain elements, or if I am, you know, enchanting my weapon with a particular type of energy, I need to specify that within the ritual itself.

## Building the generator

[0:05:51] How do we do that? We make a generator. The generator that I came up with takes a, you know, a big list of all of the verbs and nouns that it is interested in, and this list within a game might already exist in some form, if you are using some kind of action pattern, you might have all your verbs enumerated in some way along with the nouns that they can be associated with. Basically, we are going to use these to generate our set of steps. The effects we are interested in, they might require a target, or might require an element, or a magnitude which is like how powerful is the effect I am doing. First what I want to do is create associations between certain types of nouns or verbs and these elements or magnitude or what have you. Some of this might be preauthored. I might say hey, when I go to generate this, the fire rune is always going to be associated

[0:06:56] with fire because if it is associated with water, nobody is going to understand why that is. Some of it might be something we come up with on the fly, though. So if you have a whole alphabet of runes, you could say OK, the foo rune is associated with water and the thark rune is associated with air and that is a system that can change between each run of the game or however often you are generating these. The cool thing about this is players will learn this over time so you can create parallel rituals. You know, if they find a scrap of paper containing the fire bless ritual that says use the foo rune and they know the thark rune is associated with that water they might be able to figure it out, if they swap them out they can get a different effect.

[0:07:57] Part of what we need to do is create associations and pick steps to satisfy the effects for the generator.

## Execution flow

[0:08:41] We start with, you know, we have an effect we want to do like cause protection from some element and that is going to require what element and who we are protecting. Then we might say hey, you are going to speak the target's name and that will establish the target. [0:09:46] between different actions the player can take and entities within your game world. ## Interpreting actions

[0:10:29] How do we go from that to actually interpreting this within the game world? The interpreter system that I built for this is really simple. It is just taking the list of things in the previous slide and, you know, every action that you do within the game you report up to this interpreter saying hey, the player stepped north, hey the player did this or that, and we just track progress along each of these lists. I add a bit of fudging for, you know, allowing things in between because you may have to do a few intervening actions in order to draw something. If you need to like take out chalk or something it wouldn't make sense for that to disrupt the ritual. Also, not allowing it to go forever so that you can kind of distinguish between when a player is intentionally doing this, or if they happen to accidentally hit all of the milestones throughout their 10-hour run and then, you know, suddenly a spell pops off

[0:11:31] and they have no idea why. From there, we just notify whatever system you already have for doing spells to actually do the thing. So presumably you have been passing up this information to your interpreter. It says hey, the player has achieved doing this ritual. We know the information because we have been gathering it through the stuff that they have done. Now, you know, call your action thing to do whatever spell happens and let the player know that it happened. That's the what and the how. I think the more interesting is when, where, why.

## Context and narrative value

[0:12:12] Probably not combat because if you have to do a dance to cast a fireball, you are probably not going to live very long in most games but maybe. Depending on your game maybe that's viable. I think longer-term effects this makes sense. Enchanting equipment, applying buffs, healing, doing things - you might do the rituals before you go into the a dangerous room if you know there is a boss on the other side of the door and have a ritual that will protect you maybe that's worth doing. You can provide magic effects to characters who would not otherwise have them. Maybe you can allow fighter class to do the rituals. They don't normally have access to enchanting but because they found a book with the spells in it they can do some of it.
