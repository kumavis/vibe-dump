## Process

[0:03:27] FIVE THOUGHTS ABOUT PROCESS: It needs to feel accurate, not be accurate. If you want that punch to sound like a punch, you're better off snapping celery in front of a microphone than recording a boxing match. "Procedural sound generation" can mean a lot of things. Writing software to compose a sonata is no more or less legitimate than recording your own burps into your phone, putting the burp files in one folder, and then closing your eyes and pointing at the screen to pick one. (Some will disagree with me about this and they are welcome to write their own slideshow.) Collaging and mixing together pre-existing assets (that you have the right to use) until you make them your own is a legitimate way to design. Using something given away freely for you to use, for free, is not cheating. Collaborating with others, including hiring someone else in a purely transactional exchange,

[0:04:30] is also not cheating. You don’t need to do it all yourself! SECOND CONCEPT: THERE IS MORE TO SOUND DESIGN THAN JUST MUSIC AND SOUND EFFECTS, AND YOU DON'T NECESSARILY NEED TO INCORPORATE EITHER OR BOTH OF THEM. Atmospheric sound, for example, often defies either category, and can be quite simple, subtle, and powerful. The sounds of the player's environment while they are playing, including the sounds that they make themselves, are ultimately part of their experience of the design. Even if this seems like a hypothetical woo-woo exercise, it does matter. What expectations and conditions is your game made for? Consider (imagine) playing and listening to your game and design on a small ThinkPad and a set of CVS earbuds and no mouse, on a cramped commuter train.

[0:05:31] Now consider this again on a surround sound home entertainment system with a massive projection screen and a leather recliner. Now consider this again on a 4k monitor and higher-end headphones, decent mechanical keyboard, decent Staples office chair, rented bedroom. We already think about this with the more established conversation around accessibility and num pads! Same deal but for ears.

## Conclusion

[0:05:52] FIVE THOUGHTS ABOUT TECHNIQUE: Audio sprites exist, exactly the way visual sprites do. You can economically bundle and reuse a fairly small library of sounds and modify effects on the fly. "Don't mix in headphones," if you can help it, though "studio monitors" (really nice speakers specially set up for mixing) can be a luxury item. I actually have a set and still can't always use them because I don't have the physical space in my apartment. So, let's revisit this one: Mix in the best over-ear headphones you can afford or reasonably borrow, but please don't mix in earbuds. Pursue a basic grasp of frequency bands. You don't need to be able to run a fancy mixer at a live rock show. You will want to know what "noisy" and "muddy" means with a lot of overlapping audio and that you can fix it without removing them. Understand why MIDI has persisted as a technology. It's great if you deeply understand how it works, but bare minimum, try to understand why it's valuable. (Related: there's a lot of audio in the public domain that is available in MIDI form, and therefore very, very easy to make your own/adjust!) You can't always control for consistent quality. Luckily, it matters less in the overall design than consistent volume, which you can control.

[0:07:00] THIRD CONCEPT: WHETHER YOU CALL IT YOUR GAME'S MARKET OR GENRE OR TONE OR VIBE OR BIG MOOD, IT IS A PROMISE THAT YOU AS A DESIGNER ARE MAKING TO YOUR PLAYERS AND LISTENERS. Here is part of a very adaptable worksheet I originally wrote for fiction podcast writers: What does your genre setting sound like? Write down whatever sonic associations you have with ideas about the this setting or genre. There are no wrong answers! Review your list of sounds, and consider what it is that makes them feel like that genre. Imagine that tomorrow morning, our real world sounded like all of, but only, the things on your list. Are there specific types of music, machines, animals, weather, and people that are present? Why? Are there specific types of music, machines, animals, weather, and people that are absent? Why?
