## Proc Gen vs Magic

[0:02:30] And this is the slide. Beside being an excuse to use a really cool picture in a slide deck, the point I was trying to make to these Proc Gen newbies was that people tend to talk and think about Proc Gen in a way that is very similar to the positive of magic in fantasy universes, where magic is a system that is presumed to be real inside of that world. In those, magic is often appealing because it is this mysterious, fascinating thing, and it obviously grants great power to the people who know how to use it. People seem to often talk about Proc Gen in a similar way - it's this kind of magical force, where if you can understand it, you can do these great things. What people talk about less is that I think Proc Gen also has the downsides of traditional magic. It is very unpredictable because it is so mysterious, especially if you're new to it - it can do things you don't expect and that are very bad. In worlds where magic is real, you don't go to a wizard to get them to fix your fence, because they are just as likely to turn it into a sentient fence, or send it back in time, or grow it 10 feet and turn it blue.

[0:03:31] It's not a good idea just because magic exists to use it to solve every problem, and similarly, just because Proc Gen is very powerful does not mean it should be used to solve every problem. Part of why I think this happens is because Proc Gen is beguiling in a very fantasy kind of way. That promise of: you have these infinite worlds, you have endless gameplay possibilities. There is a feeling I think you can get when you are working with your generator, and you're putting in your input, you're watching it spin out content, and it feels like spinning straw into gold. It is this very appealing thing, and you can watch your generator and play with it and see what it does - and then days have gone by, and years have gone by, and there was maybe a game you were trying to make, but you don't really remember any more. Not that anyone here would know anything about that. [Laughter] ALEXEI PEPERS: This is something I like.

## Wizards

[0:06:47] Wizards. The archetypical arcane caster. The first thing most people tend to think of when they hear magic, and for good reason. The wizard is someone who thinks of Proc Gen or magic as a system of rules that can be mastered. Something that can be understood and taken advantage of. Their approach is rooted in study and understanding. If you learn all of the formula and magical sigils and everything that underlies magic or Proc Gen you can change the universe or make cool dungeon levels, same thing. The other thing about wizard is that they're prone to specialization because they view Proc Gen or magic as something they should understand fully and it tends to be that you can understand one piece better than anything else. So in the same way you have magical wizards with domain expertise in illusion, or evocation, or necromancy - you get Proc Gen wizards who have expertise in generative narrative or ecosystem simulation, or necromancy.

[0:07:49] Wizards are really powerful. They are really good to have on your side and often at the forefront of their particular Proc Gen technique. Though there are some downsides. Wizards often end up with a very arcane and incomprehensible way of speaking that is based in their deep understanding, a kind of knowledge of PCG, WFC, ECS... But don't worry, if you memorized all the characters in Rogue you can handle the acronyms. The last thing is if you have a wizard in your life, they can be prone to falling into the clutches of evil, staring too long into the abyss, filling out too many grant applications - and you will want to be ready to take them for walks in natural light and feed them home cook meals if you seen any signs of imminent turn to villainy such as sudden obsessions with jewelry or trying out spelling their names backwards.

## Sorcerer

[0:08:38] Sorcerers. The next arcane class. To me what defines a Proc Gen sorcerer is that they are centered around intuition and introspection. ## Artificer

[0:11:01] The final arcane class is the artificer. Within Proc Gen, these people filter the mind bending nature of Proc Gen into approachable interfaces. ## Warlock

[0:15:12] Finally, the warlock, the much advertised divine class.
