## Types of Choices

[0:11:21] Now that I have given you an overview of how choosing leads to fun and drama for players I want to get into the meat of the talk. The types of engaging choices in games. In this section, I will describe five different types of engaging choices mutually exclusive actions, risk versus reward, now versus later, resource trade and dilemma. I will illustrate each choice type by drawing on examples from popular traditional roguelikes. The first choice type I would like to describe is mutually exclusive action. In a mutually exclusive action choice the player has multiple things she would like to do but she want do them all. This is in essence the basic tradeoff. The player must weigh the benefits of one course of action over the opportunity cost of north naught not picking the other courses of action. A great illustration is your turn in any traditional roguelike.

[0:12:22] Typically the player only gets one action on her turn before the monster and environment take their turn. There might be several things she would like to do, drink a potion, swap her magical rings and zap the charging troll with fireballs, however she will only be able to choose one of these actions and in crunch moments where every turn counts, and one wrong move could mean permadeath, this choice can be quite tense. Another great example of a mutually exclusive choice are the treasure rooms in my favorite roguelike Brogue. These rooms contain several treasures each with their own unique benefits. Once the player takes the item off the pedestal cages drop down and she can only choose one item it leave with. There is not necessarily a write or wrong answer here. The player must assess

[0:13:23] which option to choose based on her play style, her build, what's happened so far in the game, and what she predicts might come later. The final example I am give of a mutually exclusive choice are the skill shrines in Forays into Norrendrin. These shirines come in pairs and if she accepts the benefit of one the other goes inert thus the player must think carefully about which skill she prefer to level up. People think about choices in games they often think abet about this one -- risk versus reward. The player has an option between a lower risk, lower reward and a higher risk/higher reward option. Such choices challenge the player to assess the value of a potential award

[0:14:24] relative to her ability to mitigate the risks of seeking that reward. Maybe one of the most iconic risk versus reward choices in traditional roguelike is the use of unidentified items. The player must weigh the peril of using an unknown item that might be dangerous or cursed against the direness of her situation. If the player is surrounded by monsters, is using an unknown scroll and hoping it is teleport worth the risk of it being explosive ruins instead? Another common element of traditional roguelikes that present a risk-reward choice are out of depth monsters. These enemies appear on dungeon levels much earlier than they would normally thus posing a serious threat however defeating such monsters could earn significant experience points and loot. The player must weigh whether she feels confident or equipped enough to engage with an out of depth

[0:15:28] monster or if it is better to steer clear. A third element are vaults. Chambers teaming with dangerous monsters and powerful treasures. When encountering a vault, the player must choose whether the treasures present justify the risk of engaging with a horde of tough enemies. The next type of choice I would like to describe is the now versus later choice. The player has a limited resource in these situations. If she uses it now she can't use it later. The player alternatively can have something modest or holdout for something better. Now versus later choices challenge the player to balance short term immediate needs versus or long term goals. Consumable items or items with a limited number of uses are a common example of a now versus later choice in traditional roguelikes. Items such as potions or scrolls can only be used
