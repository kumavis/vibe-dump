## Games and their traditions influence each other

[0:04:48] OK. Second point and again, this is kind of a function of being a really long-term game. Traditions build up over how to play games. People play the game, they talk about it, the developers listen to what the people who are playing are talking about, they fix bugs, they incorporate new features that people think might be interesting, and so you have this symbiosis between the game and its tradition. Angband had a particular twist on that because it was extensively modelled in the '90s particularly so there was a huge array of Angband variants so you had this constant process of people writing their on mod for Angband, releasing it as a variant, the original maintainers

[0:05:50] then absorbing bits of that back into the main game. And so you get this long, interweaving process of the game and the tradition. And there is another very interesting thing that happened with Angband specifically here where it was assumed for a very long time that the best way to play Angband was extraordinarily conservatively. It is a big, long game. You have to dive to the bottom of a 100 level dungeon and kill the main boss. It is very easy to die along the way obviously. It was accepted wisdom that you would play slowly and carefully because you really didn't want to die so it made it a very long game and that made people even more conservative because you wouldn't want to loose a month's worth of play because you did something careless or played tired or your Cat stood on the keyboard.

[0:06:56] So this was believed for a long time. Conservative play. You have got to do it. Don't go below level 20 without having free action that sort of thing. Somewhere in the mid-2000s, some players starting saying no, that's not the best way to play. It is not just boring but you are actually better in terms of number of games you complete successfully to play aggressively, to dive hard, to take down stairs as soon as you can, and to, you know, sure you will lose some characters but you can play five characters to level 20 in the same time as you used to be playing one to level 24. And so, this is an interesting example, I think, of when the tradition has actually changed gameplay considerably and that has in turn influenced the development rather
