## The heart of the genre

[0:02:55] So, I knew my goal was to make a Roguelike that captures the heart of the genre while making it accessible to folks who don't play Roguelikes which begs who is is heart of the genre? I see this as serving the goal of learning. Real, true deep learning where players are discovering for themselves the underlying rules of the games that they are practicing and failing because failure is an important part of learning but they are continuing to hone their mastery and understanding of these systems so that they can improvise in the face of whatever the generator shows at them. My initial assumptions about what I want to do to make a Roguelike more accessible to a broader audience might have been make the game easier or more tutorials to get them through

[0:04:00] the game. Looking at it as this heart of learning, you know, I really needed to protect that part of the experience so that reframed my goal to say I am trying to encourage learning and more than anything, I am trying not to interfere with learning. I don't want to interrupt the player and give them the answer when they are about to figure it out. I want to have faith in people that they can learn and give them space to experiment and I don't want to give them a false sense of progress with some kind of grain to win mechanics. I want them to come away at the end of the experience feeling like they beat the game because of the learning that they made themselves.
