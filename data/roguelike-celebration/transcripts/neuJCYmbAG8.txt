## Hunters and turn-based design

[0:10:28] Hunters, or -- the title is an acronym that stands for hell beast and vampire neutralization and tactical removal squad. Of all game jams I have done this is the one I am most eager to revisit and turn into a full fledge game. How does it work? Well, it was important for me that three things be true for the game going in. You should be able to take your time as deciding on a course of action.

[0:11:31] I most specifically didn't want there to be turn time or real-time elements that put pressure on players beyond making the best choice they can. And two turns shouldn't make other players wait for ages. This might seem contradictory but it means I want to limit the decision space enough to determine the possible courses of action could be done quickly and once an action is decided upon it should be easy to input and execute. And lastly, multiplayer should matter. I didn't want this to be a game where you could be single player who happens to have other single players around. I want to encourage collaborating and risk taking on behalf of others. How did I attain the action? Cued actions, streamline gameplay and overlapping areas that make it hard to ignore one another. To handle turns, I wanted something that would not be obtrusive but would still allow for space to think things through necessary. What I came up with is having each player input action to cue it up for execution and

[0:12:37] as soon as every player has decided on the action the turn resolves. It is easy and straightforward and easy to coordinate actions with others. ## Clean Sweep development

[0:17:27] Clean sweep is experiment I have been working on since late June based on a thought I had about how to handle multiplayer and Roguelikes. What if creatures only care about the turns of the player the most relevant to them.

[0:18:31] There is a Cat. I am sorry if you can hear that. That's how I came up with the parallel asynchronous schedule. I am going to call it that because it sounds fancy and I am allowed to make fancy terms up. Creatures don't need to care about whose turn it is on a global scale just whose turn it is on the most relevant scale. The most relevant term is that of the most nearest player. Creatures can get away with not moving when any player moves but only advancing the term when the most relevant player moves. Of course, the interesting and challenging aspect is determining what it means for a player to be most relevant but if you can manage to sus this out the result is a system

[0:19:31] where enemies align themselves to a specific player and each player takes their turn whenever they want without pressure and slowing down the game for other players. Parallel because creatures are operating on essentially separate turn schedules while still existing in the same space and asynchronous because players don't have to move in unity and can take as much or little time as you want with a situation and schedule because that's the word for organizing units of time. That's what I did in clean sweep. It worked pretty great. You can see in this clip that there are two cleaning agents wondering around and facing off and managing inventory without infringing on others or without having to wait or rush anything.
