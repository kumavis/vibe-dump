## Managing game complexity

[0:09:49] Lesson six, introduce complexity slowly. I don't want to overwhelm the player with mechanics. Until I release my game, I'm the one who has spent the most time with it. So, what's obvious to me isn't obvious to a first-time player. But if every symbol in the game has dozens of lines of explanation text on it, players will be overwhelmed and won't be able to interact with the game in a meaningful way. So, what's the solution here? Trading card games have actually already figured this one out. For a long time, a booster pack in Magic the Gathering made sure complex effects weren't printed on common rarity cards, which make up about 80% of a booster pack. So, if you open up a pack of 15 Magic cards, only three or four cards will have complex effects. By the way, Magic the Gathering's design

[0:10:49] team called this philosophy new world order if you want to read up on it more. I applied a variation of new world order to luck be a landlord. I made sure that the symbols and items with complex effects or of uncommon rarity were usually even rarer. I also made it so that uh only common symbols could appear at the beginning of a run. After the player has been offered a few symbol choices, the base rarity chances gradually increase. And since the uh the rarer symbols are also more complex, this creates a bit of a complexity curve over the course of a run, similar to the difficulty curve you'd see in a linear game.
