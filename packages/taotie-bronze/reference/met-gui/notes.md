## Why this piece

It widens the set on three counts:

1. **A new vessel type.** A round, shallow, wide bowl on a ring foot, with **two cast loop handles topped by
   sculpted animal heads**. Nothing in the set has side handles of this kind.
2. **A different décor language.** The main register is a **squared, near-flush Style IV / early V
   mask**: broad, flat bands with right-angled corners, filled and outlined with square spirals. The ground
   is the same fine leiwen, and the contrast is mostly line density rather than height. The eye bosses are
   the only high relief in the register. This is the "typographic" end of the Shang range. It is period-correct
   on this piece, so it is a useful control: the kit needs **both** this and the rounded fangding vocabulary,
   and a vessel spec must choose between them.
3. **Companion motifs that we do not yet have together on one piece:** a **rising-blade border** (蕉叶/三角纹)
   under the lip; a **dragon frieze** with a **sculpted frontal animal head** at its centre; and a foot-ring
   frieze.

The two main faces are both photographed near head-on at 4000 px, and the set also has both handle ends, a
top view and a bottom view (7 images). This is the opposite of the Met fangyi's two usable views.

## First reading of form and décor

- **Profile:** a flared lip; a short concave neck; a full, bulging belly with its widest point just below
  mid-height; a sharp inward turn to a ring foot. The foot is a decorated band over a plain stepped base ring.
  Rim diameter 159 mm, foot diameter 121 mm, belly maximum ≈ 175–180 mm (from the photo, ± 5 mm).
- **Registers, top to bottom:**
  - a plain lip;
  - a **rising-blade border**: small triangles filled with spirals, pointing up into the lip;
  - a **neck frieze**: dragons on leiwen, between thin raised borders, with a **sculpted frontal head** in
    high relief at the centre of each main face. It has a broad flat brow with two C-shaped (kidney) horns
    and round eyes;
  - the **main mask register** on the belly;
  - a **foot frieze**: dragons on leiwen, split at the centre by a short ridge.
- **Mask (each face; the handles sit on the panel boundaries):**
  - **nose ridge:** a low vertical ridge on the centre line, not a true flange.
  - **eyes:** large oval convex bosses with a short slit pupil, set low (in the lower third of the register)
    and close to the ridge.
  - **horns and brow:** above the eyes, the upper half of the register is filled by broad squared bands. These
    form a large hooked horn or brow on each side, built of right-angled turns and filled with nested square
    spirals.
  - **body and tail:** outboard, a body band ends in a squared hook.
  - **lower half:** a row of small C and S scrolls and comma hooks along the eye line. Below it, a jaw or
    claw band with square-spiral fillers.
  - **overall:** low relief throughout (bands only slightly proud, edges crisp), and corners squared rather
    than rounded.
- **Handles:** C-shaped loops cast on, each topped by a horned animal head that faces outward over the rim.
  Raised cartouche-like segments run along the loop. In end view (detail-handle-end.jpg) a long tab runs
  down from the head.
- **Seams:** the belly ridges sit on the mask centre lines. Under each handle there is presumably a second
  seam (hidden by the handle). That gives a four-part mould.

## Photo reliability

- front.jpg and back.jpg are near head-on, but the camera is slightly above the rim, so the bands curve
  downward toward the edges. To flatten them, unwrap the belly as a cylinder or barrel; the curvature is
  strong here. Trust the centre third of each face most.
- The two faces differ slightly in surface condition (face A has a darker, polished-looking left eye boss),
  not in design.
