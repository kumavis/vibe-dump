# Dossier — nezu-jia 饕餮文斝 · Jia with Taotie Design (Nezu Museum 90067)

Compact dossier in the shape of reference/met-fangding and reference/nezu-fangyi. Everything is read off the
project owner's two photographs (photo-21.jpg, photo-22.jpg; card-23.jpg is the label). **Measured** = read on
the photographs (in units of the rim diameter D); **inferred** = argued, not seen; **not visible** = no evidence.

## Label (card-23.jpg)
饕餮文斝 · Jia with Taotie Design · Shang dynasty, 13th–12th centuries BC · said to have been excavated from
Houjiazhuang, Anyang, Henan · Important Cultural Property · Nezu Museum · 90067 · exhibit 5. No dimensions.

## The photographs
- **photo-21**: near elevation, camera ≈ 4° left of the drum mask that the model calls front (θ 0), ≈ 0.4 m away,
  BELOW the rim (the rim reads as an arch seen from under: its centre higher than its ends; the far post's stem is
  hidden behind the near rim). Strong perspective: the near post's cap is 1.43–1.49 × the far one's.
- **photo-22**: three-quarter from the handle side (camera ≈ θ −44°), a little higher, ≈ 0.4–0.7 m. A neighbouring
  lidded vessel and the next label stand on the right: cropped out of every view ref.
- How azimuths were found: the handle, posts and legs were located in both photos from their projected offsets
  (x = sin(θ − camera) · r) and the near/far size ratios; both photos agree on handle −60°, legs −60/60/180, posts at
  ≈ 40° and ≈ −160° (≈ 100° either side of the handle plane, symmetric about it). A full pinhole solve on 11
  landmarks did not converge below ≈ 20 px rms (landmarks too soft; the camera is close and below the rim), so the
  cameras in the views are set by eye against the renders (compare-front / compare-three-quarter).

## Scale (inferred)
No dimension on the label and no scale object in frame (the plinth and the neighbouring vessel are of unknown size).
**Rim Ø D = 176 mm is ASSUMED**, giving 417 mm to the post tops and 322 mm to the rim — inside the 30–50 cm range of
Anyang round jia of this type (research/03 §1.5), towards the top as befits a piece said to come from the
Houjiazhuang royal cemetery. Cross-check: the drum's ground spirals then read ≈ 0.7 mm pitch (≈ 3 px in photo-21),
the fine end of the range (Met fangyi 0.65–0.75 at 24 cm) — so the jia may be smaller; ± 20 % on every absolute size.

## Registers and flanges (measured)
- Drum: one frieze 44 mm tall, THREE masks (noses at θ 0, ±120, i.e. between the legs) separated by slab flanges
  above the legs (θ 60, 180; at −60 the handle strap takes its place). Slabs ≈ 12 mm proud, ≈ 9 thick, cut into ≈ 7
  blocks (photo-21 right edge). Noses: a broad (≈ 10 mm) ladder-scored ridge, ≈ 3 mm proud.
- Waist band: ≈ 37 mm, six flat masks centred between the wall flanges (θ 30 + 60k), eyes low in the band.
- Blades: twelve rising ogive blades (30° each) from the band to just under the lip, outlined by a raised double
  line; every other one (θ 60k) is split by a wall flange; the others hold a small mask (photo-22).
- Wall flanges: six, θ 0, ±60, ±120, 180, waist → lip, ≈ 5–7 mm proud, ≈ 6 thick, T-notched at ≈ 7 mm; they
  overhang the lip with a small hook (photo-21 right silhouette). Count of notches along one: ≈ 13 (photo-21).
- Legs: notched flange on the sharp outer edge, teeth ≈ 6 mm pitch (≈ 26 per leg).
- Caps: four notched flanges at the stem corners, teeth ≈ 4.6 mm.

## Sculpted parts
- Handle: a strap ≈ 14 × 20 mm, level arm from the upper wall, C-loop down to the drum; side faces carry a border
  line and a row of cartouches (photo-21). Head at the outer top corner looking out and down: brow, bulging eyes,
  squared snout; on its crown two upright SQUARE plates each cut with a squared coil, splayed outward (photo-22).
- Posts: square stems with décor on the outer and inner faces; bell caps (flat-bottomed skirt, concave flanks,
  flared lip, low dome) with a mask on each of four flanks.
- Legs: triangular section, outer edge flanged, both outer faces carry a column of small masks for the upper two
  thirds, the back has a long channel in which red-brown / orange material shows (both photos) — read as core
  remains with cuprite; the lead's brief said "cuprite in the recesses of the legs": the photograph shows it in
  this channel, and the model puts it there.

## Relief (measured where visible, heights estimated from shading)
- Drum mask (face-drum, 7 px/mm): comb of four fat ribs ≈ 2.8 mm wide hanging from the top border either side of the
  nose; a brow bar and outer column forming an inverted U round a deep socket; the eye a pillow boss ≈ 9 × 9.6 mm
  low in the socket; a cheek plateau ≈ 12 × 8 under it; a C-hook jaw round a deep pocket at the bottom; a tall body
  column ≈ 6 mm wide near the slab flange; fine squared leiwen everywhere else. Bands ≈ 2.4 mm proud [est],
  eye ≈ 3.4 mm [est].
- Upper wall: flat bands ≈ 0.5 mm [est]; blade outlines thread-like.
- Not visible: the drum masks at ±120 (assumed = θ 0), the back of the upper wall, the inside, the bottom (a
  cast lattice or soot may exist: unknown), any inscription (jia are often inscribed behind the handle — not seen).

## Photograph vs dossier vs research
- research/03 §1.5 gives jia posts "on the side away from the handle, 70–110° apart": the photographs put them
  ≈ 200° apart, ≈ 100° either side of the handle — the photographs win.
- research/03 gives legs ≈ 0.36 H: here 0.41 of the height to the post tops (0.53 to the rim).
- The lead's brief describes the lower body as "a lower bulge"; it is a short, nearly straight drum (bulge ≈ 1.5 mm).
