# Sources: airship (a rigid airship cast as a Shang bronze)

A Taotie Kit showpiece, not a museum object. There are **no photographs** in this folder and none were used.

**Form.** A generic rigid airship of the 1920s–30s (the Zeppelin type: LZ 127 / LZ 129 and their kin), drawn from
general knowledge of the type and not from any one ship's drawings; it carries no registration, flags or insignia.
What makes it read as a rigid rather than a balloon is built in: a body of revolution 6.6 : 1 (blunt ellipsoidal
nose, fullest a third of the way back, a long convex taper to a fine tail), a 28-sided section whose bays sag a
little between the longitudinal girders, ring frames (here bowstrings), four cruciform fins with hinged
rudders/elevators, a control car hung close under the bow, four engine cars on struts with pusher propellers, a
mooring cone at the nose. Profile and proportions are written as formulas in `src/artifacts/airship/form.js`.

**Scale.** Chosen, not measured: hull 420 mm long, 64 mm across; nose toward +X, front (+Z) = the broadside; the
hull axis 150 mm above the plinth on the stand.

**Casting (the lead's design, developed here).** The hull is treated as a lathe vessel laid on its side — a gu or
zun turned horizontal — so its décor runs in ring registers: a taotie at the fullest part whose nose ridge is the
side girder (the side mould join), kui confronting that join on the fore band, cloud scrolls (云纹, the curled
companion of the thunder pattern 雷纹) on the aft band, rising cicada blades on the nose taper and hanging blades on
the tail taper, bowstrings on the ring frames, a forward-looking face round the mooring cone. The keel and side
girders are notched flanges (the mould joins); the fins are hooked fangyi-type flanges grown into plates, kui on
both faces, the rudder hinge cut through as openwork. The cars are small boat-shaped vessels (after the gong 觥)
with beast heads at the prow, the struts snake bodies, the propellers whorl bosses with blades. The stand is a
round foot with whorls and cloud scrolls, a mast with a dragon coiled up it, and a cradle whose two ends are ram
heads. All motifs are drawn for this piece with the kit's rounded vocabulary; the mask and kui adapt the
utah-teapot example's tracings-by-eye of the Met fangyi / Nezu you vocabulary; no single bronze is copied.
