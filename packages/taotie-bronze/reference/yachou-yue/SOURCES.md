# Sources: yachou-yue (亚醜钺, Shandong Museum)

Every committed photograph is Gary Todd's, published on Flickr under **CC0 1.0** (public domain dedication) and
copied to Wikimedia Commons (FlickreviewR-checked). No attribution is legally required; each file is credited
anyway. They were taken on 2012-06-19 in the Xia–Shang–Zhou gallery of the Shandong (Provincial) Museum, Jinan.

**Credit line:** Photograph by Gary Lee Todd (WorldHistoryPics.com), CC0 1.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

**Which axe it is.** (1) The label photograph `detail-label.jpg` (same camera, 11:25 that day, Commons file
"Shang Axe Inscribed with "Ya Chou" (10127586226).jpg") names exhibit 01 as 亚醜钺 "Chopper-axe Inscribed with
'Ya Chou'", 1965, Subutun M1, Qingzhou; `front.jpg` shows the same axe on a plinth marked "1"; `three-quarter.jpg`
was shot at 11:25, the same minute as the label. (2) The 亚醜 graphs are visible in the cartouches beside the
mouth in all three. (3) It is NOT the companion axe from the same site in the National Museum of China (Commons:
"20260408 Bronze Yue with Hollowed Human-Face Design.jpg", CC BY-SA 4.0, Windmemories; "CMOC Treasures of Ancient
China exhibit - bronze battle axe.jpg", CC BY-SA 2.5, Editor at Large): that one is wider than tall (published
31.7 × 35.8 cm), has small eyes in long brows, S-scroll ears, a mouth that runs almost the full width and no
T-slots in its sides. (4) "Jinan 2009 1154.jpg" (CC BY-SA 4.0, G41rn8; Shandong Provincial Museum, 2009) shows a
silvery-grey axe of this design in a case of mixed small finds — probably a cast copy or another axe; NOT used.

Originals: Commons served HTTP 429 for every original file; the largest fetchable version of each is the 3840 px
Commons thumbnail (the originals are 4752 × 3168 / 5184 × 3456 px), kept in `.work/r6-yue/originals/` (not
committed). Full views are downscaled to ≤ 1800 px (PIL Lanczos, q 88); detail crops are 1:1 from the 3840 px
version. The `faces/` images are rectified from `front.jpg`'s source (faces.json).

A second photograph the project owner supplied (the museum's press image) is NOT open-licensed: it was used only as
a private cross-check of proportions and is not committed anywhere.
