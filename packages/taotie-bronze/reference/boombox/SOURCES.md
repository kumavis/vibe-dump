# Sources: boombox (a portable radio-cassette player, dressed as a Shang bronze)

A Taotie Kit showpiece, not a museum object. There are **no photographs** in this folder and none were used.

**Form.** A generic portable radio-cassette player ("boombox") of the 1980s, of no particular make or model, drawn
from common knowledge of the type — no manufacturer's model, faceplate layout, badge, logo, brand name or model
number was copied:

- a wide, shallow box with softened vertical edges (≈ 2.2 : 1 wide to tall) standing on four short feet;
- two large round speakers at the ends of the front, each a concave cone in a round opening with a dust cap;
- between them a long horizontal tuner window with a dial scale and a needle, and below it the cassette door with its
  window and two reel hubs; a row of six piano-key transport buttons on the top front edge;
- three rotary knobs and two slider slots under the door;
- a folding carrying handle (a flat bar on two arms pivoting on the ends), shown raised;
- a telescopic antenna raised at the back right; on the back a battery door and vent slots.

**Scale.** Chosen, not measured: faceplate 464 × 209 mm (the box behind it 456 mm wide), 124 mm deep, on 12 mm feet;
speaker openings 120 mm across at ± 148 mm, cones ≈ 20 mm deep behind a sloping lip; cassette door 120 × 74 mm; the
handle's bar 26 × 12 mm, its centre line 296 mm above the floor; antenna 250 mm. Front +Z, centred on x = 0, standing
on y = 0.

**Décor.** Designed for this piece with the kit (KIT.md § Rounded bands, § Ridge work, § Coiled-serpent boss,
§ Traced plates / sunk cartouches, companion motifs), calibrated by eye on the project's Met fangyi and Nezu reference
photographs; no single bronze is copied. The casting idea (the project lead's): the object is already a taotie — the
two speakers are its eyes (coiled-serpent pupil bosses in fire-whorl sockets, ringed by fat ridges), the cassette door
its mouth (a sunk cartouche with whorl-boss reels, confronted kui on the tape path, fangs), the tuner its brow band; the
handle is a ding's upright handle made long, its arms ending in beast heads at the pivots; the antenna a jointed bamboo
rod ending in a bud.
