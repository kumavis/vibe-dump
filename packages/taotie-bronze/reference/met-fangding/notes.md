## Why this piece

This is the "showcase taotie" the round asks for. The vessel is small, so the mask is the whole face. It is
photographed **head-on on both the long and the short face** at 4000 px. It is drawn in exactly the
vocabulary the owner says our model lacks. The bands are fat, rounded and pillow-like. Each horn is one broad
flat-topped band that sweeps up and out and ends in a curled hook, with a strip of square spirals set into it.
The eye is a convex oval boss with a slit pupil. Behind the eye, the body is jointed and bends upward, carrying
hooks and quills. Under it is a bent leg with a curled claw. All of this stands in Style V-b ("smooth plateau")
high relief over a very fine, dense leiwen ground. The ground is darkened by burial, so it reads almost black
against the pale plateau, which makes tracing easy. The face is flat, so a single photo flattens to an
orthographic face with no unwrap.

## First reading of form and décor

- **Layout per long face:** a rim band (plain, thick, rolled lip); **frieze** (≈ ¼ of the décor height); a
  plain narrow fillet; the **main mask register** (≈ ¾); a plain lower margin; then the legs. A plain centre
  flange runs through the frieze and the mask and is the mask's nose ridge. Notched corner flanges with incised
  T and I marks sit on the corners. The short face repeats the scheme at a narrower width, also split by a
  centre flange.
- **Mask (each half, mirrored about the flange):**
  - **horn:** the largest element. A thick band leaves the forehead next to the flange, runs out level and
    turns up into a hooked tip at the upper outer corner. Its top edge is straight and its corners are well
    rounded. A sunk strip of square spirals runs along its length (the "incised flat top" the owner described).
  - **eye:** an oval convex boss with a horizontal slit pupil. It is about ⅕ of the half-width, sits at
    mid-height near the flange, and is ringed by a socket band with a hooked inner corner (the tear-duct hook).
  - **brow and ear:** a short crescent band above and outside the eye; a C-scroll ear outside it.
  - **body:** an upright band at the outer edge of the panel. It is jointed and carries small hooks and quills
    (dorsal spikes) on its outer side, and its tail curls in at the top.
  - **leg and claw:** below the body, a stepped, bent leg band ending in a curled claw on the bottom margin.
  - **jaw:** at the bottom centre, an upper-lip band curls outward from the flange, with an inward-hooked
    mouth corner. There is no lower jaw.
  - **ground:** fine leiwen, about 1 mm pitch by eye, which fills every gap, including inside the horn's loop.
- **Relief:** the plateau is smooth and rounded on its edges. Secondary lines are few: contour grooves and
  short hooks only (V-b). The eye boss stands above the plateau. The leiwen is flush with or below the ground.
- **Frieze:** each half-panel holds a pair of confronted dragons. Their small heads are frontal and modelled
  in higher relief (round eyes, ears), and meet at a thin divider line. Their S-curved bodies, with a round
  joint boss, stretch outward and end in large spirals, all on leiwen. These read as fat, rounded bands, not
  bars.
- **Flanges:** the centre flanges are short, plain ribs, notched at the top where they cross into the frieze.
  The corner flanges are tall slabs with two notches (at the frieze and at the top of the mask register),
  with incised T and I marks on their faces.
- **Legs:** decorated only at the top, in **incised thread lines** (Style I/II-like, not relief): a ring band,
  a hooked scroll band, then pendant blades and scrolls that fade out down the leg.
- **Handles:** plain, upright, rounded-rectangular loops with a rounded section, rising from the short-side
  rims.

## Photo reliability

- front.jpg is close to orthographic, but the camera is at body height and fairly close, so the legs splay
  slightly and the rim shows a sliver of the interior. Scale the décor from the mask register, not the legs.
- side.jpg is at a lower resolution (2000 px) but is head-on.
- The long faces are not photographed from the back, so assume the back long face matches the front, as
  usual. The second short face is not shown.
