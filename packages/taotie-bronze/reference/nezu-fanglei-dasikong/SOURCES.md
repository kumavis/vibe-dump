# Sources — nezu-fanglei-dasikong

**饕餮文方罍 · Square Lei with Taotie Design** — Nezu Museum, Tokyo (根津美術館). Bronze.

## Facts from the museum label (transcribed from the card photograph)

- Title: 饕餮文方罍 / Square Lei with Taotie Design
- Date: Shang dynasty, 13th–12th centuries BC
- Provenance: Said to have been excavated from the Dasikong site, Anyang city, Henan (伝河南省安陽殷墟大司空村出土)
- Designation: Important Cultural Property (重要文化財)
- Number on the card (lower right): 90079
- Exhibit number in the gallery: 12
- Dimensions: not on the label.
- Material: 青銅 / Bronze. Country and period line: 中国・殷時代　紀元前13～前12世紀 / China, Shang dynasty, 13th–12th centuries BC.

## The description card beside the label (Japanese; only partly in frame)

Read on card-04 and card-05 (card-35 shows only the first characters of each line). Each line is cut off at the right
edge of the frame; `[…]` marks what is not visible, a character in brackets is read from a partial glyph.

> 「罍」は酒を貯蔵する器で、肩が[張り、…]
> に向かってすぼまった胴をもつ。[蓋…]
> で大きく張り出した飾りが連なり、[…]
> 厚な印象を与えている。胴部の饕[餮…]
> や足まで全ての部位を備えた均整[…]
> で、周囲を威圧するような迫力が[…]

Working translation (of the visible words only): "A *lei* is a vessel for storing wine; it has [broad] shoulders and
a body that narrows toward [the base]. [On the lid …] strongly projecting ornaments follow one another, […] giving a
[massive] impression. The taotie on the belly […] provided with every part, down to […] and the feet, well proportioned
[…], with a force that seems to overpower its surroundings."

Used in the model: the broad-shouldered body narrowing to the base (form); "strongly projecting ornaments in a row"
read as the high, fat relief and the projecting heads (relief heights ≈ 2.3 mm plateaus, ≈ 3.2 mm eyes); "every part
… down to the feet" as the main mask carrying its bodies and the hooked feet under them (relief.js mainDesign).
