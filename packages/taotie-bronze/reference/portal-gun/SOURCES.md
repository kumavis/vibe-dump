# Sources: portal-gun (a portal gun cast as a Shang bronze)

A Taotie Kit showpiece, not a museum object. There are **no photographs** in this folder and none were used.

**Form — an original design.** The owner asked for "a portal gun". The device here is designed from that idea alone —
a hand-held instrument that throws a ring-shaped doorway — and is based on **no existing prop, film, game or series**.
No version of the idea from any game, film or television show was looked up, traced or recalled for it, and it carries no
names, logos, numbers, colour schemes or signature silhouettes from any of them. Its parts follow only the generic
grammar of a hand-held sci-fi emitter: a wide circular mouth at the front that plainly throws a ring, a core chamber
visible from the sides, a body/barrel with cooling fins, a one-hand grip with trigger and guard, a plug-in power cell
with a cable, a selector dial. Proportions are written as numbers in `src/artifacts/portal-gun/form.js`.

**Scale.** Chosen, not measured: the device 378 mm from the power cell's cap to the emitter's lip (401 mm with the cap's
ring handle), the emitter 140 mm across; muzzle toward +X, so the front view (+Z) is the side profile; the barrel axis
175 mm above the plinth on its stand.

**Casting (the project lead's design, developed here).** The emitter is the trumpet mouth of a gu: rising blades with
cicadas outside, four notched flanges reduced to three (the bottom is left to the cradle), and inside a stepped funnel
whose treads carry pairs of bowstrings — the concentric rings of the portal — round a domed throat bearing the fire
whorl (囧纹), under a circle band (圆圈纹) on the lip. The body's two flanks each carry half a taotie drawn in ridge
work (fat multi-ridge figures on plateaus, squared spirals in every cell, the method of the kit's Dasikong-blade
specimen): its eye is the power core — an almond socket holding a turquoise lens behind a bronze bar that is both the
slit pupil and the cage; the ear is the selector dial. The barrel carries a register of kui on leiwen between
bowstrings; the cooling fins are notched ring flanges whose notch rhythm does the work of vents. The grip is the handle
beast of a guang: a horned tiger whose head bites the body, whose scaled body is the grip (belly plates for the
fingers), whose tongue is the trigger and whose tail curls forward into the guard. Shang founders inlaid turquoise in
bronze; the core lenses, the dial's stone and the power cell's windows are turquoise inlay (a stone material, not a
patina, not lit). The stand is a base slab with cloud scrolls in a ridge border, two posts, and two confronted
crouching rams whose hollowed backs are the cradle. All motifs are drawn for this piece with the kit's vocabulary;
no single bronze is copied.
