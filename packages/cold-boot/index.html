Cold Boot

Every time an x86 PC starts, its processor wakes up pretending to be a 1978 Intel 8086, promotes itself to a 1985 386, borrows its own cache as memory while it teaches the DDR5 how to talk, becomes a 2003 AMD64 — and then does the same dance on every other core.
