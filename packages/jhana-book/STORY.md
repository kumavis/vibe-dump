# Jhāna Book — telling the story without paragraphs

A proposal for giving each spread *context* (why this state, in this order) and
*method* (how you get there) while keeping the pages as quiet as they are now.

## What the research says the story is

**The jhānas are a ladder of letting go, not a ladder of effort.** The suttas
introduce every state by naming what was released to reach it:

| state | the sutta's entry phrase (DN 2, Bodhi) | what you let go of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | "secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states … rapture and pleasure born of seclusion" | the outside world |
| 2nd | "with the stilling of thinking and examining" | inner commentary |
| 3rd | "with the fading away of rapture" | the thrill |
| 4th | "with the abandoning of pleasure and pain" | pleasure itself |
| space | "with the complete surmounting of perceptions of form … 'space is infinite'" | form, edges |
| consciousness | "surmounting the base of infinite space … 'consciousness is infinite'" | the space |
| nothingness | "'there is nothing'" | the knowing's content |
| neither | "surmounting the base of nothingness" | even that |

That is already the physical story of this book: **turning a page folds the
previous scene flat.** Every turn is a release. We only have to say so, once,
and then let the pages carry it.

**Why these states.** They are the Buddha's own definition of right
concentration, the last limb of the eightfold path (SN 45.8), and the pleasure
he decided was "not to be feared" (MN 36). They are not the goal: on emerging,
Sāriputta reviewed each state's factors "one by one as they occurred" (MN 111)
and insight followed. The four formless states are *bases* (āyatana) in the
suttas rather than jhānas; calling them jhānas 5–8 is a later convention the
book can quietly admit.

**How you get there, in the fewest words.** From Brasington's instructions
(leighb.com, *Right Concentration*):

- Access: sit upright and at ease; watch the breath at the nostrils until it
  thins and thoughts go "wispy"; don't take a deep breath to find it again.
- 1st: "shift your attention … to a pleasant sensation" and "do nothing else";
  "the jhāna will arise on its own"; "don't try to do the jhānas, you can't".
  Pīti is physical (shivers, heat, light); sukha is gladness. It is not calm.
- 2nd: shift "from the physical pleasure to the emotional pleasure, from the
  pīti to the sukha". Thinking stops. "Drenched, steeped."
- 3rd: "letting go of the physical pleasure and changing the emotional pleasure
  from joy to contentment, almost like turning down the volume".
- 4th: "a bit more letting go": contentment refines into "neutral, equanimous,
  quiet stillness"; a feeling of "sinking down, down, down".
- Space: find "the boundaries of your being" and "push these boundaries
  outward" (the room, the building, the city) until "a sudden shift" into "a
  huge expanse of empty space".
- Consciousness: "to gaze at an infinite spaciousness you must have an
  infinite consciousness"; turn attention to that.
- Nothingness: shift attention "from the infinite consciousness to the content
  of that consciousness", and find none.
- Neither: "letting go of all the outward, infinite expanse and coming to rest
  in what seems to be a very natural calm quiet place"; "the mind knows more
  about how to find this than can be verbalized".
- After: come back down through the states, then review what was there.

The similes (DN 2) are the images the scenes are already built from: the ball
of bath powder moist through yet not dripping; the lake fed only by its own
spring; lotuses that never rise above the water; a man sitting covered head to
foot in white cloth.

## Design principle

Tell it the way a pop-up book tells anything: **in the mechanics, in the
margins, and behind a tab.** Never more than one new sentence on screen at a
time. Everything below is layered so each layer can ship on its own.

### Layer 1 — the hinge line (what was let go)

One small italic line on each **left** page, under the Pāli, in the sutta's own
subtraction phrase. Because the left page is the back of the leaf that just
turned, the reader sees this line *during the turn*, as the old scene folds
down. The mechanic and the sentence say the same thing at the same moment.

| spread | hinge line |
| --- | --- |
| intro | *(none; the frontispiece, below)* |
| jhana-1 | *born of seclusion* |
| jhana-2 | *with the stilling of thought* |
| jhana-3 | *with the fading of rapture* |
| jhana-4 | *with the abandoning of pleasure and pain* |
| jhana-5 | *beyond form* |
| jhana-6 | *beyond space* |
| jhana-7 | *beyond consciousness* |
| jhana-8 | *beyond nothing* |
| outro | *emerging* |

Cost: a field per scene and one line in the page texture. This is the layer
that answers "why these, in this order" with ten words total.

### Layer 2 — the guide's whisper (how to be here)

While you rest on a spread, short second-person lines appear **one at a time,
one per breath cycle**, printed into the paper where the right page's line
sits now (the fixed line is simply the first of them). Each fades in over an
in-breath and stays until the next replaces it. Three to five lines per spread,
never more than eight words, in the register of the instructions above. After
the last line the page goes quiet and stays quiet; the reader can sit for an
hour. A small lamp icon in the HUD turns the guide off; `?guide=0` too.

The lines, in order (first line = the current printed line):

- **intro.** Sit. Let the breath find you. · Upright, at ease. · The breath,
  at the nostrils. · Thoughts go wispy. Let them. · The breath thins. Don't
  deepen it. · Something pleasant. Stay there.
- **jhana-1.** Rapture rises, born of letting go. · Stay with the pleasant.
  · Don't help it grow. · It arrives on its own. · Tingling, heat, light.
  Let it. · Thoughts still mutter. Ignore them.
- **jhana-2.** A spring wells up from within. · Turn from the thrill to the
  gladness. · Thinking falls quiet. · Nothing comes in from outside. · Soaked
  through.
- **jhana-3.** Lotuses, drinking the cool water. · Let the thrill drain away.
  · Contentment remains. · Turn the volume down. · Mindful. Clearly knowing.
- **jhana-4.** Still. A white cloth over everything. · Let even the contentment
  go. · Neither pleasant nor unpleasant. · The breath, almost nothing.
  · Covered, head to foot.
- **jhana-5.** Space, without edge. · Find the edges of yourself. · Push them
  outward. · The room. The city. The sky. · The edges give way.
- **jhana-6.** Knowing, without edge. · What knows the space? · Turn toward
  the knowing. · It has no edge either.
- **jhana-7.** There is nothing here. · What does the knowing hold? · Nothing.
  · Rest in that.
- **jhana-8.** Almost nothing. Almost. · Let the vastness go too. · A natural,
  quiet place. · Too fine to call it perceiving. · Don't look. It is here.
- **outro.** Come back slowly. Carry the quiet. · Down the way you came.
  · Calm, then gladness, then the breath. · What was there? Look again.
  · This is where insight begins.

Cost: a `guide` array per scene; in the engine a caption plane on the right
page (a transparent canvas texture, redrawn once per line, cross-faded), a
breath-cycle counter per spread, the toggle. No change to the scenes.

### Layer 3 — the scene enacts the line (optional per spread)

The engine passes `frame.beat` (index of the line currently showing). Scenes
may stage themselves by beat so the picture and the instruction move together:

- intro: the orb only appears at "Something pleasant"; before that the pond
  reflects nothing.
- jhana-1: the fan is a low glow at beat 0; rays rise at "Stay with the
  pleasant"; sparks begin at "Tingling"; faint drifting marks at the page
  edges (the muttering thoughts) dim at the last line.
- jhana-2: the outermost ripple fades at "Nothing comes in from outside"; the
  spring brightens at "Soaked through".
- jhana-3: blossoms open fully at "Contentment remains"; the caustics slow at
  "Turn the volume down".
- jhana-4: the light sweep halts at "Still"; nothing else.
- jhana-5: the tunnel's far glow recedes one arch per line.
- jhana-6: at "Turn toward the knowing" every jewel lights together once.
- jhana-7: the printed thread fades at "Nothing"; the mote stops drifting.
- jhana-8: the veils thin by one step at "Let the vastness go too".
- outro: the window light warms per line; the birds lift at the last.

Cost: a few lines in each scene's `update`; one agent per two spreads, or one
agent for all ten since the changes are small.

### Layer 4 — the pull tab (the simile and the source)

Pop-up books have pull tabs. Each right page gets a small paper tab at its outer
edge. Pulling it (click, or drag, or the `i` key) slides a strip out from under
the page carrying **one or two sentences**: the canonical simile for the form
jhānas, the formula for the formless bases, with the source in tiny type. Push
it back and the page is clean again. Nothing is on screen unless asked for.

- intro — *Attention settles on one thing until the breath goes faint and
  thought thins. Some teachers describe a light that appears here: the nimitta.*
  (Brasington; Ajahn Brahm)
- jhana-1 — *Like a bath attendant kneading soap-powder with water until the
  ball is moist through and through, yet does not drip.* (DN 2) *Rapture is
  bodily: shivers, heat, light. The first jhāna is not a calm state.*
- jhana-2 — *A deep lake with no inlet from any side, fed by a cool spring
  welling up from within, until no part of it is unsuffused.* (DN 2)
- jhana-3 — *Lotuses born in the water, grown in the water, that never rise
  above it, drenched from tip to root.* (DN 2)
- jhana-4 — *A man sitting covered from the head down by a white cloth, no part
  of him uncovered.* (DN 2) *From here the Buddha turned the mind to insight.*
- jhana-5 — *"Space is infinite." Not attending to form or to diversity. In the
  suttas the formless states are bases, āyatana, rather than jhānas.* (MN 111)
- jhana-6 — *"Consciousness is infinite." To behold boundless space, the
  knowing must be boundless; attention turns to the knowing.*
- jhana-7 — *"There is nothing." Attention turns from the knowing to what it
  holds, and finds nothing there.*
- jhana-8 — *Perception too subtle to call perceiving, too present to call its
  absence. The mind finds this place better than words do.*
- outro — *On emerging, review the state: what arose, how it passed. Sāriputta
  did this one by one.* (MN 111) *A concentrated mind, turned to seeing.*

The tab can also be a real mechanism: pulling it feeds the scene a `frame.pull`
value (0–1) so the spring wells harder, the fan opens wider, the veils thin.
Pop-up tabs move things; that is why people pull them.

Cost: a tab mesh and a sliding card per spread with a canvas text texture; a
hover/press affordance; an engine field `tab` per scene. The scene reaction is
optional.

### Layer 5 — frontispiece and colophon

Books say what they are on the first page, in a few lines, and where they came
from on the last. Both belong on the covers' insides, which the book already
has:

- **Frontispiece**, a pasted bookplate card high on the inside front cover
  (the intro's left page), in four short lines:
  *Eight states of absorption.* / *The Buddha called them right concentration,
  the pleasure not to be feared.* / *Each is entered by letting go of the last.*
  / *Breathe with the ring. Turn when something has been released.*
- **Colophon**, small, on the inside back cover (the outro's right page):
  *Similes from the Sāmaññaphala Sutta, DN 2. Instructions after Leigh
  Brasington, Right Concentration. The formless bases are counted as jhānas
  five to eight by convention.*

That is the only place more than one sentence appears together, and it is
where a reader expects it.

## What this does not do, on purpose

- No paragraphs on the pages, no side panel, no "about" screen, no voice-over.
- No claims about attainment. The lines say what to attend to and what to let
  go of; they never say "you are now in the second jhāna".
- No factor diagrams. The five jhāna factors were considered as a small printed
  ledger per page; it is accurate but it turns the page into a chart, and the
  hinge line already carries the same information in the sutta's own words.
- No timers. The breath ring is the only clock; lines arrive on breaths.

## Suggested order

1. **Layers 1, 2 and 5** together: a text-only pass (scene metadata + engine
   captions + the two cover cards). Most of the value, no risk to the scenes.
   One agent for the engine, text supplied from this document.
2. **Layer 4**, the pull tab: one engine agent for the mechanism, text from
   this document.
3. **Layer 3**, scene beats and tab reactions: one or two scene agents, after
   the engine passes `beat` and `pull`.
