# Battles: what the four battle files follow

The battles workflow (ENGINEERING.md §13) wrote `public/data/boxes/{midway,
ironbottom-sound, samar, pearl-harbor}/battle.json`, the ledger
`public/data/sources-battles.json` and the check `tools/check/battle.mjs`.
This note says, battle by battle, which record each track and event follows,
how each source clock was converted, which tellings ride along, what is
schematic and why, what is still unsettled, and what needs a person. The
research behind it is R4 (Midway's air battle), R5 (the two surface battles)
and R10 (Pearl Harbor); where this note and those disagree, the battle file and
this note are the later reading.

The files were generated by small scripts kept out of the package (they are
scaffolding; the JSON is the deliverable). Every number in a file was put there
by hand from a source or computed from one, and each fix or event names the
source it follows.

## Conventions in all four files

**Fields beyond DATA.md §9's example**, each one validated by
`tools/check/battle.mjs` and each one needing a line in DATA.md (a note for the
contract's owner, below):

- `sites[]` with `kind` from `airfield | hangar | ramp | seaplane-base |
  drydock | quay | aa | fuel-tanks | radar | building | barracks | hospital |
  command-post | landmark`; `building`, `barracks`, `hospital` and
  `command-post` always `target: false`. Sites carry `events` (bombs on a
  hangar line, strafing of a ramp) with `by` and a count range, like units.
- `unit.offBox: true` for ships that never come into the box (the Kidō Butai
  at Pearl Harbor); they are exempt from the box-holds-tracks check, as an
  off-box group's `from` already is.
- `station.bearingRef: "true"` on every station: bearings are true, not
  relative to the guide's head (NWC's relative stations were converted).
- `station.parked` with its own `lonlat` (within 3 km of the site) for each
  parked aircraft, so they stand in lines.
- `group.wave` (1 or 2) at Pearl Harbor, which the check uses to keep the
  first wave's level bombers out of every role.
- `group.lossesBy` (Midway: who shot down whom, as ranges, where the record
  says), `group.launchSource` (the launch time as the source printed it),
  `route[].precision/source/note`.
- `moment.checkpoints[0]` (time and text at `t + durationS × timeScale`),
  `record.weapon` on a hit, `record.damageTaken`, `record.claims`,
  `record.rounds`, `record.torpedoes`, `record.track`.
- `event.byNote` (why the `by` was chosen when the record does not name the
  shooter), `event.note`, `unit.kitNote` (a ship or aircraft drawn with a kit
  of a near class), `hangarHazard[].sources/note`.
- Fates use `sunk | scuttled | capsized | beached | damaged | undamaged |
  withdrew | lost | returned`; a `sunk`, `scuttled` or `capsized` fate with a
  time has a `sinks`, `explodes` or `scuttled` event at that minute.
- Objective kinds: `dive | torpedo | level | gunnery | helm | intercept | cap
  | escort | sortie | aa`.
- `sides` is keyed `japan` / `usa` as DATA.md's example, each with `side: "jp"`
  or `"us"`, the code every unit and group carries.

**The no-numbers rule.** Counts, casualties, rounds, ranges and positions live
in typed fields and `tellings`. Notes, stop text, beats and after-text carry
none: "several", "most", "within minutes", "a pair". The check holds every
text field to it (DATA.md §10), with an allow-list widened in `battle.mjs` for
designators (B-17, I-168, SBD-2s), calibres (37-mm) and "No. 2" turrets.
Objective text is exempt from the digit rule but not from the people rule
(DATA.md's own example says "thousand-pound").

**`by` where the record names no shooter.** The authority rule needs a cause
for every scripted hit. Where the record does not name one, the box picks the
unit the record places in range and in action at that minute and says so in
`byNote`. Two rules override that: a fixed unit's events are never caused by a
group or unit that a moment makes the player's role (Arizona, Oklahoma, Utah),
and a group's scripted hits never exceed what `attack.reached` says reached the
release.

**Precision.** `report`: a position the record gives in degrees and minutes.
`chart`: read off a public-domain chart (±2′ unless noted) or an OSM feature.
`narrative`: placed from bearings, ranges and descriptions in the text.
`schematic`: placed by the box where no record gives a position, to the
record's constraints; every schematic fix's `sourceNote` says what constrained
it.

## Midway, 4 June 1942

**Clock.** Midway time, zone +12 (UTC−12), 04:00–18:00, as CINCPAC and the
Naval War College keep it. The Nagumo report keeps Tokyo time and the Tokyo
date: local = JST + 3 h, one day earlier (01:30 JST 5 June = 04:30 local
4 June). Yorktown, Enterprise, Hornet and their squadrons kept zone +10:
local = their time − 2 h. Every reading from either carries `sourceClock`,
`sourceZone` and (for Tokyo) `sourceDate`; the check recomputes each.

**Tracks.** Akagi: NWC 1948's fix at 07:00 (170 miles 315° from Midway), its
Diagram D-3 for 09:00 and 10:22, Nagumo's own 10:00 signal (30°N 179°W). The
other carriers keep station on Akagi before the attack (R4 D2's constraints;
no source gives the spacing) and each has her own fixes after it; Hiryū's run
north-east from 10:54 is D-3/D-4. The support force and screen keep station.
Yorktown's track is NWC's fixes (stopped 12:40–13:40 after the bombs, under
way, torpedoed 14:44, abandoned); TF 17's screen is NWC p. 137's
relative-to-axis disposition converted to true bearings. Enterprise is NWC's
track; Hornet keeps station on her (045°, 8 km). Pensacola, Vincennes, Balch
and Benham have tracks to TF 17 from 12:37, joining at 14:05.

**Events.** Japanese side from the Nagumo report's log and tables; US side
from CINCPAC, the carriers' reports and NWC; R4 §3 for the attacks minute by
minute. Hits per carrier follow NWC and the Nagumo report with Parshall and
Tully, the TROMs and ja.wikipedia as tellings (Kaga's four hits and the
bombers' order; Akagi's one hit, Best's; Sōryū's three; Hiryū's four). Sites
(Eastern and Sand Islands: airfield, powerhouse as a building, command post,
AA, seaplane hangars and base, fuel tanks, Battery D, dispensary as a
hospital, cable station) follow CINCPAC and Heinl 1948, with OSM for the
islands' outlines. Kidō Butai at 10:20 is schematic, as the contract says.

**Tellings carried.** The bomb hits on each carrier; the scuttling times (Kaga
19:25, Sōryū 19:13, Akagi and Hiryū the next morning); Nautilus's torpedoes;
the B-17s' and Marines' claims against Nagumo's "no hits"; Hiryū's second
strike launch (12:45, 13:20–13:31 or 13:30); Yorktown's torpedo hits in three
clocks; the losses by unit (Torpedo Eight; Hiryū's strikes; Tomonaga).

**Unsettled.** The frozen Midway bounds (ids.json, DATA.md §7) end at 183.0°
(177°W): TF 16 and TF 17 operated at 176°14′–176°50′W, up to 74 km beyond the
east edge. The check warns (33 warnings) rather than fails. Recommended
bounds: west 179.3°E, south 27.8°N, east 184.0° (176°W), north 32.2°N.

**Needs a person.** The Japanese carriers' relative positions at 10:20 (a
reader of Senshi Sōsho 43 or Parshall and Tully's diagrams); JACAR's carrier
action reports read in Japanese.

## Ironbottom Sound, 14–15 November 1942

**Clock.** Zone −11 (UTC+11), 20:00 to 03:30, as Morison V, ONI Part I and the
TROMs keep it; times after midnight are written 24:xx. Washington's and South
Dakota's reports, the South Dakota war damage report, ONI Part III and
Lundgren's essays keep zone −12: local = their time − 1 h (0100 zone −12 =
24:00 here). Japanese reports keep Tokyo time: local = JST + 2 h. One official
narrative is thus on two clocks (ONI Part I and Part III); every beat from a
zone −12 source carries its own reading, which the check enforces by source.
South Dakota's own chronology runs ten minutes off Washington's (Morison n.
58): carried as a telling at the searchlight beat.

**Tracks.** Washington: ONI's track chart (p. 63, public domain) at its
corners and the turns, ONI's narrative for 20:10–21:05, Morison's times
(21:48, 22:52, 23:35, 00:20, 00:33). Morison's n. 54 fix (9°20′S 159°27′E)
and ONI's 23:00 position (13.5 miles SE of Savo's south point) disagree with
the chart by miles; both are in `sourceNote` and the chart wins. South Dakota:
2,000 yards astern until her power failure, then 2,000–4,000 yards north-east
of Washington (Lundgren's figure, R5 §3.4) and her own 235° and 215° from her
report. The four destroyers run in Washington's track, ahead of her, closing
or opening evenly from 23:20 to the places where Washington passed them
(Preston 23:35, Walke 23:41), which is where the chart has them burning.
Kirishima: schematic from the north to 23:51, then ONI's 24:00 bearing and
range from Washington, the chart's loops 24:04–24:15, her capsizing position
from the TROM. Kondō's other ships, the screening unit and the sweeping unit
are schematic (R5 G2), the columns sampled from their leaders; Sendai's loop
east of Savo is the chart's.

**Tellings carried.** Kirishima's 16-inch hits (Lee 8, USSBS about 9, Morison
9 of 75, ONI about 8, the 2019 survey 17–21) and 5-inch (17–40); South
Dakota's hits (26 in the WDR, 42 in Morison) and her dead (30, 38, 39); the
time Kirishima capsized (03:20 Morison, 03:25 TROM, Lundgren's "0323" on zone
−12); Walke's sinking time; Preston's port-side heavy shells (ONI's unseen
cruiser against Lundgren's friendly-fire argument); the whole two nights'
losses.

**Schematic and why.** No Japanese track was drawn by any source opened before
23:51; the Japanese ships are placed to R5's constraints. Ayanami's speed is
in no source read. Torpedo spreads by Kimura's group are one event per half
(the split by ship is not in what was read).

**Unsettled.** Whose torpedoes hit Walke and Benham (Nagara's destroyers or
Ayanami); what sank Preston. Ships that withdraw out of the box (Washington,
South Dakota, Benham, Gwin, Atago, Takao) end their tracks inside it with a
`withdraws` event; the engine has to decide what a unit does after its last
fix (a note for ENGINEERING.md §5).

**Needs a person.** Washington's and CTF 64's reports and track charts (NARA
RG 38), or a reader with Lundgren's book; Senshi Sōsho 83 for the Japanese
dispositions.

## Samar, 25 October 1944

**Clock.** Item (zone −9, UTC+9), 06:15 to 10:45, as the American reports,
Morison XII and the Japanese reports keep it (Tokyo time is the same hour).
The early entries of the Yamato, Kongō, Haruna, Chōkai, Haguro, Yahagi and
Noshiro TROMs run one hour behind Item (R5 §2.2): each such reading carries
its printed time with `sourceZone: "+08:00"` (the Yamato TROM's "0544" for the
06:44 sighting). The clock ends before the Shikishima unit's attack at 10:50.

**Tracks.** Taffy 3 follows the *Battle Experience* no. 22 track chart (TU
77.4.3, p. 27, public domain), carried by Fanshaw Bay, with Morison's turns
(east at 06:55, 110° at 07:23, 200° at 07:29, 170° at 07:32, south at 07:46,
south-west by 08:00). The other carriers hold the 08:16 order (Gambier Bay's
diagram via Morison) all morning: the order on the circle before then is in
nothing read. Gambier Bay falls astern from her first hit at 08:10 to where the
chart has her capsize. Kurita's disposition at 06:44 is Plate 9-3 (USSBS Nav 9,
Kurita's own sketch, public domain), schematic; Morison's different version
is in the notes. Tone follows the chart's "Enemy CA" track from 07:32 to 09:00
(she led the cruisers by 08:49, Haguro's report via Morison); Chikuma runs
ahead of her to 08:20, then closes Gambier Bay. Yahagi follows the chart's
"Enemy light forces" at 07:15 and 09:25. Johnston's run is worked from her
report: 18,000 yards with the leading cruiser on about 345° at 07:10, inside
10,000 yards with a target angle of 040 at about 07:20, and the torpedo's run
(R5 §4.6) meeting Kumano at 07:27. Hoel's run is placed from her report
(9,000 yards from the leading battleship on 140° at 07:27). Everything else is
schematic.

**Events.** US side from the Johnston and Hoel reports, *Battle Experience*
(CTU 77.4.3 and 77.4.2, Fowler's report) and Morison XII; Japanese side from
the TROMs and USSBS interrogations. The air attacks are Morison's and the
TROMs': Suzuya near-missed at 07:35, Haguro's turret at 08:00, Chikuma's air
torpedo at 08:53 (Tone's report via Morison), Chōkai hit by White Plains at
08:51 and by Kitkun Bay's Avengers at 09:05 (Fowler).

**Tellings carried.** Johnston's torpedo hit on Kumano (07:24 TROM, 07:27
Morison; bow or stern); the 07:30 hits on Johnston (her report's battleship and
light cruiser; Lundgren 2014's Yamato); Gambier Bay's attackers and Tone's
rounds; Hoel's "more than forty" hits; Roberts's torpedoes (evaded, or one
hit); Chōkai's end (sank about 09:30 by Haguro's chart, scuttled 21:48 by the
TROM); the losses of each ship as Morison, the reports and the Association
give them.

**Unsettled.** Which of DesRon 2's destroyers were present (all seven in R5's
list are in the file, with the caveat); Taffy 2's ships are not units (they
stayed out of gun range except for splashes astern of its destroyers) and its
strike enters at the south edge; Chikuma's own 09:30 position (126°48′E) is a
telling, being far east of the battle's track.

**The St. Lo beat.** The Shikishima unit is a group with `tokko: true` whose
entry (10:46) and attack (10:50) fall after the clock; St. Lo carries the hit
at 10:51, the explosions and her sinking at 11:25; the closing stop at 10:45
carries R8 §5.2e's card, with "kamikaze" beside "tokkō".

**Needs a person.** JACAR C08030037100 (CruDiv 7's detailed report and charts)
read in Japanese; someone with Morison's Samar chart beside the *Battle
Experience* chart to decide whether the battleships' tracks after 07:30 can be
drawn from public-domain sources alone.

## Pearl Harbor, 7 December 1941

**Clock.** Hawaiian time, UTC−10:30, 06:30 to 10:00 (tz database; Monaghan's
"1821 GCT (0751 LCT)"). Japanese records: HST = JST − 19 h 30 min (03:25 JST
8 December = 07:55 HST 7 December). The Bishop's Point radio log keeps GCT:
HST = GCT − 10 h 30 min. Ward's messages (06:51 and 06:53) carry their GCT
readings.

**Anchorage.** ComBatShips' berths and pairs; F-6 to F-8 and the Arizona and
Utah wrecks from OSM (`chart`); F-3 to F-5 inferred (`schematic`, R10 D14);
outboard ships one beam to the channel side of the quay line (052°–232°).
Drydock No. 1 from OSM; YFD-2, 1010 Dock and every other berth (the destroyer
nests in East and Middle Loch, the Navy Yard berths, Ford Island's far side)
schematic from the narrative. Nevada's sortie follows CINCPAC, her report and
Kimmel's narrative (F-8 at 08:40, off Drydock No. 1 about 08:55, aground at the
turn off Hospital Point at 09:10). Helm's track is her report's times; Ward's,
Monaghan's and the midgets' are schematic around the record's events.

**Air groups.** Both waves by carrier from USSBS App. 3, the TROMs and the
JACAR first sheets (183 and 167 flown), entering at the north edge at 07:28
and 08:30 (R10 §3.4); routes from the AAF history's maps and the USMC
monograph. Targets as the record has them; reached and losses from Exhibit 8
and the TROMs (29 lost: Kaga's torpedo unit five of twelve, Shōkaku one D3A in
the first wave; fourteen D3As and six A6Ms in the second). The Kidō Butai is
off the box (R10 D1) at the launch points computed from Exhibit 8 and JM-97.

**Damage authority.** The BuShips *Summary of War Damage* (R10 D5) for hits per
ship; ComBatShips, CINCPAC, the ships' reports and the NPS as tellings. The
`by` choices where no record names the unit: Oklahoma's torpedoes to Akagi's
unit and Arizona's fatal bomb to Hiryū's level bombers (the Hiryū TROM; ja.wp
credits Kaga's), so that no fixed fate has a player's role as its cause; Utah's
to Sōryū's (ja.wp).

**Fixed, hospital, tokkō.** Arizona, Oklahoma and Utah carry `fixed: true`;
Solace `hospital: true, target: false`; the five midget submarines `tokko:
true` (R10 D9), with the one Helm fired on a target only of Helm's `sortie`
objective.

**People and places never targets.** Hickam's barracks, Wheeler's tents and
the Naval Hospital are sites with `target: false`; the strafing of people stays
on cards. The parked aircraft (Hickam's B-17Ds, B-18s and A-20As, Wheeler's
P-40s and P-36As, Kāneʻohe's PBYs) are units in lines; the record's losses by
field are tellings on the sites.

**Kits.** Ships with no kit are left out and listed here: the submarines,
Dobbin and Whitney, Medusa, Rigel, Ramapo, Argonne, Sumner, Castor, Pelias,
Sacramento, the minesweepers, Avocet, Swan, the tugs, Pyro and Widgeon; the
fleet submarines of the Advance Expeditionary Force. Near-class kits (with
`kitNote`): the Bagley class with `gridley` (the PHA list classes them so),
the flush-deck DMs, DMSs and AVDs and Allen with `wickes`, Abukuma with
`nagara`, B-17Ds with `b-17e`, P-40Cs with `p-40b`.

**Unsettled.** Which carrier's torpedoes struck which battleship; which group
dropped Arizona's bomb (Prange and Zimm, not opened); Nevada's 08:30–08:40
bomb hits; the time of the first strafing at Kāneʻohe; Shaw's explosion (about
09:30 against Mugford's 10:05).

**Needs a person.** A georeferenced 1941 chart of the harbour for the berths
and channels; the berthing plan (ComBatShips' Enclosure A); a Japanese reader
for the JACAR air group records; the card wording on the dead (R8 and a
historian).

## The check, `tools/check/battle.mjs`

It runs under Stage 0's runner on the four frozen boxes and on the fixture's
test box, and holds each file to DATA.md §9 and §10 as the task listed: the
clock and its required other clocks (Midway's two, Guadalcanal's two, Samar's
TROM hour, Pearl Harbor's Tokyo and GCT), every `sourceClock` recomputed, the
sources known to keep another zone forced to carry one; tracks inside the clock
and the box (a warning, 10 km margin); stations without cycles; carriers'
`hangarHazard`; fixed, hospital and tokkō rules; the Samar clock ending before
the St. Lo beat; groups' `reached` within `count` and scripted hits within
`reached`; the moments' roles (never a person, a tokkō unit, a hospital ship,
a fixed unit's cause or Pearl Harbor's first-wave level bombers), windows,
checkpoints, starts, objectives, records and after-cards in R8's words; stops;
the copy, voice and no-numbers rules; and, when `routes.json` exists, a route
of the event matching the units' tracks at the start.
