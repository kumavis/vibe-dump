# R4: the carrier and air battles, as material for choreography

Researcher R4, for the designer of the Pacific War diorama. Written 2026-10-22.
Topic: the record of the carrier and air battles a player can enter. Midway
(4 June 1942) in full. Pearl Harbor (7 December 1941), Ten-Gō (7 April 1945),
the Philippine Sea (19 June 1944) and Santa Cruz (26 October 1942) in outline.

This file follows the brief's §6 and the Hawaiian standard (`RESEARCH.md`):
every number sits in a telling with its source; where tellings disagree, each
is given; anything not opened is marked *verify*. "Opened" means R4 fetched and
read the text in this pass. R2's ledger (`R2-sources.md`) covers the same
corpus as a catalogue; this file mines it for choreography and does not repeat
R2's catalogue work except where a number is needed.

Downloads (small, under `research/downloads/R4/`): the HyperWar texts of the
Nagumo report and its eight tables and three charts, the US Midway action
reports, the NWC 1948 OCR text and two of its fold-out diagrams (D-3, D-4),
the combinedfleet.com TROMs for Akagi, Kaga, Sōryū, Hiryū and Yamato, the
Pearl Harbor CINCPAC report, two AAF chapters, USSBS *Campaigns* ch. 2, the
TF 58 Okinawa report, JM-90 and five chapters of the ONI Santa Cruz narrative.
Scratch scripts (HTML to text, sun position, bearing/distance) are in
`research/r4-scripts/`; nothing they made is needed to use this file.

## 0. Headline findings

1. **The Midway record is good enough to choreograph minute by minute from
   primaries on both sides.** Nagumo's own action report (ONI Review, May 1947,
   HyperWar) carries a signal and event log at one-minute resolution, a table of
   every attack on every ship with bombs, hits and sinking positions, the CAP
   and AA tables, the strike organisations and the weather over each target.
   The US carrier reports, CINCPAC's report and the Naval War College analysis
   of 1948 (archive.org, opened, with its plotted diagrams) give the American
   side. §3.7–3.8 are the result: 04:30–17:05 local, about 110 timed beats.
2. **Three clocks, one minute.** The dive on Kaga is 0722 in Nagumo's report
   (Tokyo time, 5 June), 1222 in the Enterprise report (zone +10) and 10:22 in
   NWC and CINCPAC (Midway local, zone +12). Convert JST by **+3 h and −1 day**,
   US carrier time by **−2 h**. R2 found the rule; every beat here is converted
   and carries its source clock (§2). Several US reports were written "from
   memory" and run 20–40 minutes off the logs (Yorktown's own report is the
   worst; §3.9).
3. **TF 17's formation at noon is in the record to the yard.** NWC 1948:
   Yorktown at the centre; Portland 60° on the starboard bow and Astoria 60° on
   the port bow on the 2,000-yard circle; Morris 340°, Hammann 020°, Russell
   120°, Hughes 240°, Anderson 180° on the 3,000-yard circle; 30.5 knots; axis
   225°. The Yorktown report adds the starboard automatic battery (ten 20 mm,
   four 1.1-inch mounts, twelve .50 and two .30). That is a complete scenario
   for the D3A strike and the F4F CAP (§3.2, §3.11 M7–M8).
4. **The Kidō Butai's box at 10:20 is not in any source reachable here.** The
   primaries fix only relative positions: Kaga 30° to port of Akagi at 10:20
   (Akagi TROM); Sōryū east of the two (NWC; VB-3 report puts her "to the North
   Eastward in the formation" as seen from the south-east); Hiryū ahead to the
   north under cloud and separated after dodging VT-3 (ja.wikipedia citing
   Japanese works); battleships and cruisers "as much as 12 miles" from the
   carriers, which "appeared to be closely escorted by destroyers only" (NWC on
   VT-6's approach, 09:40). The diagram everyone cites is Parshall and Tully's
   (*Shattered Sword*, 2005, print). The box must be drawn as **schematic**
   until a person reads that book and Lundstrom (§3.2, decision D2).
5. **The famous disagreements are documented in the primaries themselves.**
   Bombs on Akagi: two hits and a near miss (Nagumo 1947) or one hit and two
   near misses, the "aft hit" exploding in the water by the port quarter and
   jamming the rudder (Parshall and Tully, through the Akagi TROM and
   Wikipedia). Bombs on Kaga: four of nine (Nagumo), "a minimum of one 1,000-lb
   and three 500-lb" (Kaga TROM), "three to five" (Wikipedia). Sōryū: three
   (Nagumo, NWC, TROM) against VB-3's claim of five to seven. The order: Kaga
   first (10:22–10:24), Sōryū (10:25–10:28), Akagi (10:26) in every log read.
   NWC 1948 has VS-6 and VB-6 attacking two different carriers; the later
   telling (and Best's own) has both squadrons diving on Kaga and only Best's
   three-plane section on Akagi (§3.9).
6. **"The torpedo planes pulled the Zeros down" is a 1942 telling, kept by NWC
   1948, and argued against since 2005.** Spruance (CTF 16, 16 June 1942) and
   NWC both say it in so many words; the dive-bomber reports agree only that
   there was "no fighter opposition at altitude". Parshall and Tully (via
   Wikipedia and a forum paraphrase, *verify*) put the failure in the CAP's
   control and position (drawn to the south-east by VT-3) rather than its
   altitude. The app can show the record (Zeros low in the south-east, none at
   altitude over Kaga) without asserting the cause (§3.9 item 1).
7. **The arming-change story has a primary core and a later accretion.**
   Nagumo's log: 07:15 "re-equip yourselves with bombs"; 07:28 Tone 4's
   contact (delivered about 08:00 by the report's own account, which NWC finds
   inconsistent); 07:45 "leave torpedoes on those not yet changed". The
   report's readiness signal puts CarDiv 1's strike ready at **10:30** and
   CarDiv 2's at 10:30–11:00. Fuchida's "five minutes from launch with decks
   full of armed planes" (1955) is not in the report; an Akagi CAP pilot's
   memory (Kimura, via ja.wikipedia) has only fighters on her deck at 10:26
   (§3.9 item 2).
8. **The NWC 1948 diagrams are public domain and drawable.** Diagrams D-3
   (09:00–13:00) and D-4 (13:00–24:00) are plotted tracks for both sides with
   timed points. Read by eye (±5′), they put the 10:22–10:28 dive at about
   30°10′N 179°01′W, Yorktown's noon position at about 30°52′N 176°50′W,
   the torpedo hit at 14:41 at about 30°43′N 176°43′W and Hiryū's hit at 17:05
   at about 31°34′N 178°27′W. They also show that **CINCPAC's and Fletcher's
   "33°51′/33°52′N" for Yorktown is a misprint for 30°5x′**, and the Yorktown
   report's "167°-24′ W" sinking longitude for 176°24′ (§3.3).
9. **Attack profiles come from the targets as much as the attackers.** Akagi
   saw Best's section at about 2,000 m in a dive of about 50°, releasing at
   about 500 m (Nagumo). VB-3 pushed over from 14,500 ft out of the sun, dived
   from the south along the fore-and-aft line, released at an average of
   2,500 ft (Yorktown report). Yorktown watched Hiryū's D3As glide to a 60°
   position angle at 6,000–8,000 ft, steepen, and release at about 500 ft;
   Juneau watched Shōkaku's at Santa Cruz dive at 70–80° and release at about
   800 ft. TBDs flew at 100 knots at about 1,500 ft below the cloud base and
   went down to 150 ft under attack; the Mark 13 then had to be dropped at
   about 110 knots and 50 ft (NavWeaps). The B5Ns released at about 800 yards
   from Yorktown (CINCPAC) (§3.6).
10. **Casualty figures differ by source and by what they count; per-ship
    figures differ by up to 20 %.** Akagi 221 (Nagumo) or 263 (TROM); Kaga
    printed "1 800" in the 1947 translation, 814 (TROM); Sōryū 718 or 713;
    Hiryū 416 or 417 (the latter including 34 later rescued). Japanese total
    2,500 (NWC) or 3,057 (Parshall and Tully, *verify*); US about 307. Japanese
    aircraft 234 (NWC) or 248 (Parshall and Tully, *verify*); US 132 to about
    150 (§3.10).
11. **VT-8 should not be a playable sortie.** All fifteen were shot down; one
    torpedo is known to have been dropped; none hit; 29 of 30 men died. There
    is no sortie-terms success the record allows, and the moment would end in
    the player's death in almost every run. Offer it as a stop with a card, and
    let the CAP-against-VT-3 moment (Japanese side) and Thach's escort (US
    side) carry the low-altitude fight (§3.11 M4, decision D3).
12. **The outline battles each have one clean moment and one to avoid.** Pearl
    Harbor: Welch and Taylor's first P-40 sortie from Haleʻiwa (about 08:30,
    over ʻEwa) and Nevada's sortie down the channel (08:40–09:10) are clean
    defence roles; the record of both is in the AAF history and CINCPAC's
    report (opened). Ten-Gō: the first wave at 12:32–12:45 (Yamato's helm and
    AA, or a TBM of Hornet's five from 70° to port) is fightable; the final
    VT-9 run on a ship already on her beam ends at 14:10 should not be offered.
    Philippine Sea: an F6F against Raid II, intercepted about 60 miles out.
    Santa Cruz: Widhelm's SBDs on Shōkaku (10:30–10:50) and Shōkaku's D3As on
    Hornet (10:10); the damaged aircraft that crashed into Hornet and Smith are
    record beats, never a player's choice (§4–§7).

## 1. Method

1. Read the brief, the Hawaiian `DESIGN.md` §8 and `RESEARCH.md` §10 and §12
   (the step-by-step choreography and the tone rule: no gore, no bodies; the
   rule practised, not announced), and R2's ledger for what was reachable.
2. Fetched the Midway primaries on HyperWar with curl and a User-Agent;
   converted HTML to text; read the Nagumo report and its tables in full, and
   the US reports in full; converted every time to Midway local.
3. Fetched the NWC 1948 OCR text (archive.org `_djvu.txt`, 827 KB) and read
   pp. 84–98 and 120–143; located and viewed its diagrams D-3 and D-4 through
   archive.org's page-image service; read positions off them by eye.
4. Fetched the TROMs for the four carriers and Yamato (combinedfleet.com,
   copyrighted compilations: finding aids and tellings, not the record).
5. Read the Japanese and English Wikipedia articles (wikitext via
   `action=raw`) for the Japanese-language tellings they cite and for the
   Parshall-and-Tully positions they summarise. These are tertiary.
6. For the outline battles: CINCPAC's Pearl Harbor report, the AAF's *7
   December 1941: The Air Force Story* chs. 4 and 6, USSBS *Campaigns* ch. 2,
   the TF 58 report for March–May 1945, the Yamato TROM, JM-90 (A-GO) and the
   ONI Combat Narrative for Santa Cruz.
7. Computed sun altitude and azimuth for each candidate moment (NOAA
   fractional-year algorithm); checked it against NWC's Midway sunrise (0457
   local; computed 04:54) and converted reported bearings and distances to
   latitude and longitude on a sphere.

### 3.2 Formations and stations

**Kidō Butai.** What the opened record fixes:

- Before the morning attacks: "Screen cruising disposition #1. Course, 130
  degrees" (log, 0030 JST); "Assume Air Alert Disposition #1, Method B" (0350
  JST, 06:50 local); "Battleships in 10-8-3 order. Course 70, speed 12"
  (blinker, 0630 JST, 09:30 local). The geometry of these dispositions is not
  in the translation.
- Individual ships manoeuvred independently under air attack: "Individual
  maneuver under air attack appears to have been standard Japanese procedure
  or doctrine" (NWC p. 88).
- At 09:40 (VT-6's view): "a loose formation, with battleships and cruisers as
  much as 12 miles away from the carriers, which appeared to be closely
  escorted by destroyers only"; course about 270°, then 000°, then 180° to keep
  the torpedo planes on the quarter (NWC p. 129–130).
- At 10:20: "AKAGI sights enemy dive-bombers about to dive on KAGA which is
  currently 30 degrees to port" (Akagi TROM). Fleet course had been set to
  300° at 10:10 to put sterns to VT-3 (Akagi TROM; log 0714 JST "Akagi settles
  on course 300").
- McClusky approached from the south-west after following Arashi north-east
  (NWC p. 131; Wikipedia). NWC has him take the carrier on his port bow and
  send VB-6 to the one on his starboard bow (p. 131); Wikipedia (from
  Lundstrom and Parshall and Tully) has Best "headed north to attack Akagi".
  So Akagi lay north (or north-east) of Kaga.
- Sōryū: "the third carrier, which was to the east of the two attacked by his
  own planes" (NWC p. 132); VB-3, approaching from the south-east, found its
  target "located to the North Eastward in the formation" (Yorktown report).
- Hiryū: "ahead of the other three carriers, under the clouds" when VT-3
  arrived (ja.wikipedia citing Cressman et al., *A Glorious Page*, as
  「ヨークタウン」1994, p. 188, *verify*); separated further by her evasion of
  VT-3 and not attacked by the dive bombers (same, citing 『飛龍の生涯』
  p. 397). Wikipedia (citing Parshall and Tully pp. 330–353) says instead that
  VT-3 attacked Hiryū "hemmed in by Sōryū, Kaga, and Akagi". These are not
  easily reconciled; Parshall and Tully's diagram decides (*verify*).

**Recommendation:** draw the four carriers in a box about 5–8 km on a side
(*verify*: no source read gives the spacing) with Kaga south-west, Akagi north
of Kaga, Sōryū east, Hiryū north-east and drawing away; a destroyer close to
each carrier; Haruna, Kirishima, Tone, Chikuma and Nagara out on the ring at
10–20 km. Mark it `schematic` in the data, with the constraints above in its
note, until someone reads *Shattered Sword* ch. 10–11 and its appendix, and
Lundstrom's *First Team*.

**TF 17 at the noon attack (exact, NWC p. 137).** "Circular anti-aircraft
disposition with the YORKTOWN in the center, the cruiser PORTLAND 60° on the
starboard bow, and the cruiser ASTORIA 60° on the port bow. The MORRIS was in
position 340°, the HAMMANN 020°, the RUSSELL 120°, the HUGHES 240°, and the
ANDERSON 180°. The cruisers were on the 2000 yard circle; the destroyers on the
3000 yard circle. Speed was 30.5 knots; the axis 225° (T)." The Yorktown report
says "radius of screen one mile, speed 25 knots" before the attack, raised to
"about 30½ knots" with "radical turns". The bearings are relative to the
formation axis (225°) in US practice of the time (*verify*); if so, Morris is
at true 205°, Hammann 245°, and so on.

**TF 17 at the torpedo attack.** Pensacola, Vincennes, Balch and Benham had
joined from TF 16 (CINCPAC; Fletcher: at "1530" zone +10, 13:30 local); course
and axis about 090° when the disposition formed (NWC p. 139); Yorktown at
17–20 knots, launching fighters.

**TF 16.** Each carrier in its own circular screen, "AA formation 11V"
ordered at 08:35 (Midway time); the two groups were "separated at times by as
little as several thousands of yards, and at times by as much as 20 miles"
until 19:30; doctrine of the time split carriers before an expected air attack
(NWC p. 123). Fletcher directed the two forces to operate "five to ten miles apart. Task
Force SIXTEEN to Southwestward" from 04:20 (0620 zone +10; ComCruPac report,
opened).

### 3.9 Where the tellings diverge

1. **How many Zeros were low, and why the dive bombers met none.**
   - *Spruance, 16 June 1942:* "The wiping out of our torpedo plane squadrons
     was, I believe, done largely by enemy VFs. This seems to have pulled enemy
     VFs down and left the air clear for our dive bombers."
   - *CINCPAC:* "the VT squadrons were a sacrifice that enabled the dive
     bombers to make their attack almost unopposed".
   - *NWC 1948 (p. 127):* "the Japanese brought most, if not all, of the
     defending fighters down to low altitudes in order to concentrate maximum
     effort against the more greatly feared American torpedo planes, leaving
     few if any fighters to oppose incoming dive bombing attacks."
   - *Gay, 1943 oral history:* the torpedo squadrons "sucked those fighters
     down".
   - *The dive-bomber record itself:* "There was no fighter opposition at
     altitude" (VB-3); McClusky "encountered no fighter opposition and little
     anti-aircraft fire" (NWC). It says where the Zeros were not, not why.
   - *Japanese record:* the Hiryū CAP log has its fighters fighting VT-3 and
     its escort at 10:09–10:18; Akagi was launching fighters at 10:26; the
     CAP's own claims (Kaga 32, Hiryū 46, Sōryū 28 and 4 probables, Akagi "522
     (totals do not check)") are wildly inflated.
   - *Parshall and Tully (2005), via Wikipedia and a forum paraphrase
     (verify):* the CAP's failure was one of control and position: VT-3
     "quickly drew the majority of the Japanese CAP to the southeast quadrant";
     a Zero could regain 10,000 ft in about five minutes, and some 45 minutes
     separated VT-8's attack from the dive.
   - *VT-3's survivor Harry Corl, via ja.wikipedia:* "most of the Japanese CAP
     was concentrated on Yorktown's fighters and torpedo planes, fighting at low
     altitude", 24 Zeros by his count.
   - *Counts by witness:* VF-3: 18–20 Zeros; VT-3: "at least 8"; VT-6: about
     25 (NWC); Gay: about 35.
   **For the app:** draw the CAP in the south-east at low altitude over VT-3
   from 10:10, none over Kaga at 10:22, and a few fighters launching; let the
   card give both readings.

2. **The arming change and the "five minutes".** Primary: the 07:15 order, the
   07:28 report "delivered about 0500 [08:00]" (Nagumo narrative; NWC calls the
   delay inconsistent with the 07:45 order), the 07:45 counter-order, the
   08:30 order for 250-kg bombs on CarDiv 2's dive bombers. Readiness:
   "CarDiv 1 advised that its ship-based attack planes (torpedo equipped)
   would be ready for the take-off at 0730 [10:30] and CarDiv 2 ... from 0730
   to 0800 [10:30–11:00]" (narrative, p. 7). Spruance: the dive bombers
   "caught one enemy CV (Akagi) with most, if not all, of his planes on deck".
   VB-3: Sōryū's "flight deck was covered with planes spotted aft ... A fighter
   was blown over the side as it was being launched." Kimura (Akagi CAP, via
   ja.wikipedia): only his fighter launching and a second Zero on her deck.
   Wikipedia (Parshall and Tully pp. 231, 121–124): the planes on deck were
   CAP fighters; spotting a strike would have taken at least 30 minutes.
   Fuchida (1955) said the decks were full and launch was five minutes off
   (print, the telling Parshall and Tully refute; R2 lists it). **For the
   app:** at 10:22 the decks hold a few fighters (Sōryū's spotted aft per
   VB-3); the hangars hold armed and fuelled aircraft and loose ordnance; the
   card quotes the 10:30 readiness signal.

4. **The order of the attacks on Kaga and Sōryū.** Every log read puts Kaga
   first: Akagi sees Kaga dive-bombed at 10:22 and fires on her at 10:24;
   Sōryū's hits 10:25–10:29; Akagi's at 10:26 (Nagumo, NWC "1022 Kaga, 1025
   Akagi, 1028 Soryu" on D-3, TROMs). ja.wikipedia notes "some American
   researchers" who have Leslie's VB-3 attack Kaga (*verify*: the work is not
   named). VB-3's description of its target ("vertical smokestacks encased in
   one ... on the starboard side") fits none of the four exactly.

5. **Which squadron took which carrier.** NWC 1948: McClusky led VS-6 on the
   carrier on his port bow, VB-6 took the one on his starboard bow; VB-6's
   second division then joined the VS-6 target and its third attacked both.
   Wikipedia (Lundstrom; Parshall and Tully): a miscommunication sent both
   squadrons onto Kaga, and only Best and his two wingmen pulled out and went
   north to Akagi. The later telling matches Akagi's own count of three
   attackers.

6. **VT-8's target.** "The southernmost carrier" (NWC); "probably hitting the
   Kaga" (CINCPAC, of Gay); Gay (1943) calls "the larger one close to me" the
   Akagi. Nagumo's log notes only the force's firing and evasions at 09:18–
   09:36.

7. **When Hiryū's second strike left.** 13:31 (NWC; D-4); "1031 Second attack
   wave takes off from Hiryu" (log) and "The second attack wave took off at
   1020" (CarDiv 2's report in the log) = 13:20–13:31; but the log also has
   "0906 Second attack wave takes off from Hiryu" (Chikuma's entry), which
   matches nothing else; the Hiryū TROM gives 12:45; ja.wikipedia 13:30.

8. **Yorktown's own times.** Its report was "compiled entirely from memory";
   it puts the torpedo hits "about 1620" (14:20 local), 23 minutes before every
   log; Fletcher's report puts them at "1541" z+10, evidently a misprint for
   1641. Use NWC and CINCPAC.

9. **The Hornet air group's track.** The carrier's report says the group took
   239° for 155 miles and turned south. Later tellings (Symonds, Mrazek; print,
   *verify*) argue the bombers flew a more westerly course ("the flight to
   nowhere"). The squadron track charts (Hornet report Enclosure F) were not on
   HyperWar.

10. **Kaga's end.** "The fire on board spread to both the forward and aft fuel
    tanks by 1625 [19:25], causing two great explosions and the ship's sinking"
    (Nagumo). The Kaga TROM (Tully): Hagikaze fired two torpedoes into her
    starboard side at 19:25 on orders to scuttle. Sōryū likewise: Nagumo has her
    sink at 19:13 with "a great underwater explosion" at 19:20; the TROM has
    Isokaze's three torpedoes at 19:12.

11. **Positions.** Kaga's sinking latitude differs by 3′ within the Nagumo
    report itself (text 30-20.3N, Table 3 30-23.3N); Sōryū's differs by 31 miles
    between Nagumo and the TROM; Hiryū's attack position by about 40′ of
    longitude between the Enterprise report and NWC's D-4. Yorktown's "33°"
    and "167°" are misprints (§3.3).

12. **The prisoner's statement.** Nagumo's log and NWC record what a captured
    Yorktown flier told DesDiv 4. The 1947 translation withholds the names
    ("purposely omitted") and says he "died on 6 June and was buried at sea".
    How the US prisoners at Midway died is not in any document read here; it is
    a card matter for R8 and a person, never drawn (§3.12).

### 3.11 Candidate moments for the player

Each block: role; start clock and length; start state; what the enemy does;
the beats that happen regardless; the card's after-text (what the record
says). Distances in nautical miles, altitudes in feet, speeds in knots unless
marked. "Verify" marks numbers taken from doctrine or memory rather than the
record. Sortie score is always in sortie terms (hits, aircraft downed,
torpedoes evaded, came back), never in people.

**M1. VS-6 / VB-6 on Kaga (US, SBD-3).** *Recommended for v1.*
- Role: an SBD of Enterprise's group, McClusky's lead division or the VB-6
  division that followed onto Kaga.
- Start: 10:21:30, length 3–4 minutes.
- Start state: 14,500–19,000 ft (NWC: approach begun at 19,000 ft at 10:10),
  1–2 miles south-west of Kaga, heading about 045°, in a step-down vee in
  column; sun high in the east-south-east (68°, az 106°).
- Enemy: Kaga under way at high speed, turning to starboard (Kaga TROM),
  Akagi to the north, Sōryū to the east, Hiryū far to the north-east;
  CAP fighters low in the south-east over VT-3; AA slow to start ("little
  anti-aircraft fire", NWC); no fighter at altitude.
- Beats: 10:22:00 the first three bombs miss; 10:22:30–10:24 bombs four, seven,
  eight and nine hit (aft starboard, forward elevator twice, amidships); fire
  at 10:24; three SBDs peel off north (Best); 10:25 Sōryū hit to the east;
  10:26 Akagi hit to the north; retirement low "through gaps in the Japanese
  screening force", first toward Midway (NWC).
- After-text (draft): "Nagumo's report counts nine dive bombers on Kaga at
  10:22: three bombs missed, four hit. One destroyed the bridge. Kaga burned
  through the afternoon and sank at 19:25. VS-6 and VB-6 lost fourteen
  aircraft that morning; four to seven came down at sea with empty tanks on
  the way home." Plus the
  ranges from §3.10.
- Score: release height, hit or miss, pull-out above 1,000 ft (*verify*
  minimum), reaching Point Option (fuel; ditching is the commonest end in
  the record).

**M2. Best's section on Akagi (US, SBD-3).** *Recommended for v1.*
- Role: Lt Richard H. Best (VB-6), or a wingman (Kroeger, Weber).
- Start: 10:25:00, length 2–3 minutes.
- Start state: above and to the south of Akagi after pulling out of the
  approach to Kaga (*verify*: Best's altitude after the pull-out is not in a
  source read); attack "from position bearing 80 degrees to port" of Akagi.
- Enemy: Akagi in a hard reverse turn, fighters starting to launch; Kaga
  burning to the south-west.
- Beats: 10:26 bomb one a near miss about 10 m off the port side forward;
  bomb two hits at the amidships elevator; bomb three (Nagumo: hit aft;
  Parshall and Tully: under the port quarter); 10:29 torpedoes in the hangar
  explode.
- After-text: "One bomb, probably Best's, was enough. It burst among armed and
  fuelled aircraft in the upper hangar. A near miss astern jammed the rudder.
  Nagumo left the ship. Akagi was scuttled at dawn on 5 June." Akagi's dead,
  221–263.

**M3. VB-3 on Sōryū (US, SBD-3).** *Recommended for v1.*
- Role: an SBD of VB-3 (Leslie's own bomb had fallen off early; the second
  plane, Holmberg's, made the first hit, ja.wikipedia).
- Start: 10:24:30, length 3 minutes.
- Start state: 14,500 ft, out of the sun to the south-east of Sōryū; dive from
  the south along her fore-and-aft line.
- Enemy: Sōryū turning right to a southerly course to launch fighters; no
  fighters at altitude; "The first plane to dive received no anti-aircraft
  fire" (NWC); then heavy light AA.
- Beats: three hits 10:25–10:28 (forward elevator; abaft No. 2 elevator;
  amidships); a fighter blown over the side; the last four planes shift to a
  destroyer and a battleship (Isokaze near-missed astern; no hits); withdrawal
  north-east close to the water; release at about 2,500 ft.
- After-text: "Three bombs hit. Sōryū's engines stopped within fifteen minutes
  and her captain ordered her abandoned at 10:45. She sank at 19:13. None of
  VB-3's seventeen planes was lost over the target." Sōryū's dead, 713–718.

**M4. VT-8's run (US, TBD-1).** *Not recommended as a sortie.* Record: launch
07:00–08:06; 100 kt at about 1,500 ft below the clouds; carriers sighted to the
north-west about 09:20; two divisions (four two-plane sections; one three-plane
and two two-plane, NWC p. 128); Zeros from 09:28 about 8 miles out; all 15
shot down; one torpedo known dropped; no hit. The record allows no sortie-terms
success: any hit would contradict it, and the player's death would end almost
every run. Offer it as a tour stop with a card (the approach line on the sea,
the squadron's fifteen aircraft named on the card as aircraft, the after-text
from the Hornet and Enterprise reports), and let M5 and M6 carry the
low-altitude fight. Never draw the man in the water (tone rule; Gay's view of
the burning carriers is a card quotation at most).

**M5. Hiryū's CAP against VT-3 (Japanese, A6M2).** *Recommended, pending R8.*
- Role: a Zero of the carriers' CAP (Hiryū's own, by the log that records
  this fight).
- Start: 10:09, length 4–5 minutes (to 10:14); or start 10:17 and run to
  10:22 so that the dive begins overhead.
- Start state: low (1,000–3,000 ft, *verify*), south-east of the carriers,
  turning toward AA bursts fired by a screening ship to mark the raid
  (Yorktown VF report); 12 TBD at 150 ft, 14 miles out, splitting into two
  divisions of six for an anvil on Hiryū (ja.wikipedia); six F4F of VF-3 above
  them (two at about 2,500 ft, four at 5,000–6,000 ft), weaving in pairs.
- Beats: Thach's division turns into the attackers; Bassett's F4F is lost;
  VT-3 loses seven on the approach; five drop; Hiryū combs the tracks; no hit;
  then, unseen behind the player, the dive bombers arrive over Kaga at 10:22.
- After-text: "Hiryū's fighters claimed nine torpedo planes and all nine
  escorting fighters. The record: ten of twelve torpedo planes and one of six
  fighters lost; no torpedo hit. At 10:22, while the fighters were low in the
  south-east, dive bombers fell on Kaga, Akagi and Sōryū." Zero losses in this

- Score: torpedo planes turned away before the drop; torpedoes kept off your

**M6. Thach's escort over VT-3 (US, F4F-4).** The mirror of M5, 10:05–10:12:

**M7. Hiryū's D3A strike on Yorktown (Japanese, D3A1).** *Recommended, pending R8.*

- Start: 11:59, length 4–5 minutes (to the release at about 12:05 and the

- Enemy: TF 17's CAP of 12 F4F plus TF 16's (about 28 in all) vectored out

- Beats: the formation is broken; "one at a time, seven planes were seen to

**M8. The F4F CAP over Yorktown (US, F4F-4).** *Recommended for v1.*

- Beats: as M7 from the defender's side; at 12:05 the hits.
- After-text: "The combat air patrol broke up the attack 15 to 20 miles out.
  Seven bombers got through. Yorktown's fighters claimed thirteen bombers and
  two fighters; Japanese records count thirteen bombers and three fighters

**M9. Tomonaga's torpedo strike on Yorktown (Japanese, B5N2; or US, F4F-4).**
- Role: a B5N of Hashimoto's second chūtai (all five returned; it made the
  hits), or of Tomonaga's first (all lost). Or the F4F defence.
- Start: 14:38, length 4–5 minutes.
- Start state: at about 5,000 ft (US fighters at 10,000–12,000 ft "over-ran"
  them, Yorktown report), 10–14 miles from TF 17, descending into a loose vee at
  50–2,000 ft; ten B5N, six A6M; the formation splits to attack from both

- Enemy: Yorktown at 17–20 kt, launching her last eight fighters with 23

- Beats: Thach shoots down a B5N with a leader's marking, which drops as it

  14:43–14:45 two hit the port side abreast the bridge; Yorktown loses power

- After-text: "Ten torpedo planes attacked; five were lost, with Tomonaga.
  Two torpedoes hit. Hiryū believed she had disabled a second carrier.
  Yorktown was abandoned within half an hour and sank on 7 June after a

**M10. The dive on Hiryū (US, SBD-3).** *Recommended as a later moment.*
- Role: an SBD of VS-6 or VB-6 (Gallaher's group) or of Yorktown's VB-3

- Start: 17:00, length 3–4 minutes.
- Start state: approach from the west-south-west with the low sun behind (22°,

- Enemy: Hiryū, Haruna, Tone, Chikuma, Nagara and three destroyers gathered

### 3.12 What the tone rule touches at Midway (for R8)

- The deaths of captured US fliers aboard Japanese destroyers (the prisoner in
  Nagumo's report "died on 6 June and was buried at sea"; names withheld in
  1947). Not drawn; a card only if a person who has read the sources decides.
- Men in the water (Gay; Kaga's and Sōryū's men blown overboard; Yorktown's).
  Not drawn.
- The CAP's and the dive bombers' "kills" are aircraft, scored as aircraft.
- The Nagumo report praises Hiryū's crews who flew "body crash" attacks (Part
  VI). The Santa Cruz narrative calls crashing aircraft "suicide" planes (§7).
  Neither is a tokkō attack in the 1944–45 sense; the cards should say what
  happened (a damaged aircraft crashed into the ship), not what the 1942 writer
  called it.

**Approach and timing (USSBS).** "The approach was made at an altitude of
3,000 meters above a dense cloud layer which was hanging about 2,000 meters."
First wave over Oʻahu about 07:40, ordered to attack at 07:50: dive bombers on
Wheeler 07:55, torpedoes on the battleships 07:57, fighters on grounded
aircraft 08:00, horizontal bombers on the battleships 08:05. Second wave sighted
Oʻahu 08:40, ordered to attack 08:55, attacked about 09:00. "Both attack units
attacked for 30 to 60 minutes and then rendezvoused with their respective
fighter units at a point bearing 340° distance 20 miles from Kaena Point."

**CINCPAC's phases (opened).** 07:55–08:25 torpedo and dive-bomber attacks;
08:25–08:40 a relative lull; 08:40–09:15 horizontal bombers at about 12,000 ft
(photographed), with dive bombers; 09:15–09:45 dive bombers; "All enemy planes
had retired by 0945." The report warns: "There are great conflicts in these
reports."

**Routes (AAF, *7 December 1941: The Air Force Story*, 1991, ch. 4, opened).**
The first wave formed up north of Kahuku Point, turned west along the north
shore and split near Haleʻiwa: Fuchida's horizontal bombers and Murata's
torpedo planes toward Kaʻena Point, then south, west of the Waiʻanae range,
splitting again to come in from the west and south; Takahashi's dive bombers
down the centre of the island with fighters above. Over Wheeler they divided:
Sakamoto's 25 on Wheeler (from west and east, all staying east of the
Waiʻanaes, not through Kolekole Pass as witnesses thought), Takahashi's 26 on to
Hickam and Ford Island. Two float planes from Tone and Chikuma flew ahead to
scout Pearl Harbor and Lahaina. The AAF history's maps 6 and 7 ("Japanese
Aircraft Deployment, First Attack" and "Second Attack") are on HyperWar
(`AAF/7Dec41/maps/7Dec41-6.jpg`, `-7.jpg`; not opened).

**Torpedo attacks (CINCPAC, Phase I).** Four attacks, about 21 planes: 12 "from
the Southeast, over the tank farm, Merry Point, and environs; launching their
torpedoes from very low altitudes, and at very short distances, toward the
battleships on the South side of Ford Island"; 3 from the south-west across
Drydock No. 1 at California (F-3), Oklahoma (F-5) and West Virginia (F-6); 1
from the west at Helena (with Oglala outboard, at 1010 Dock); 5 from the
north-west at Utah, Raleigh, Detroit and Tangier. Dive bombers over Battleship
Row pulled out "at about 300 feet".

### 4.3 The P-40s from Haleʻiwa (AAF, ch. 4, opened)

- 2d Lts George S. Welch and Kenneth M. Taylor, 47th Pursuit Squadron,
  drove from Wheeler to Haleʻiwa, where the squadron was deployed for gunnery
  practice and the Japanese had not struck. Ground crews had P-40s armed.
- **First sortie**, "around 0830": ground control sent them to "the southern
  tip of the island where the Japanese from the first wave were still strafing
  the Marine base at Ewa. Spotting a group of enemy planes in a long line, both
  pilots jumped into the line and began shooting down aircraft, each getting
  two confirmed kills during this first engagement. Taylor fired on a third
  plane but did not see the crash." Out of ammunition, they landed at Wheeler.
- **Second sortie**, around the time Sanders's P-36s fought over Kāneʻohe
  (about 08:50 on): Welch took off first; Taylor took off into "another
  Japanese attack" on Wheeler, firing at the last of the line; a Japanese
  aircraft got on his tail; Welch shot it down (his third). Taylor, wounded,
  kept fighting. Welch went back to ʻEwa for a fourth.
- Fourteen Army pilots engaged; Sterling was lost in combat, Whiteman and
  Bishop on take-off at Bellows, Dains to friendly AA. The AAF puts the Army
  score at 10 confirmed (11 with Dains's probable).
- Not in the record read: the P-40s' altitudes and the type of the "long
  line" (D3As and A6Ms rendezvousing after the attack on ʻEwa, *verify*). The
  first wave's rendezvous point (340°, 20 miles off Kaʻena Point) lies north
  of Haleʻiwa, which is why the departing aircraft were near the north shore.

### 4.4 Nevada's sortie (CINCPAC, opened): a battleship-helm moment

"At 0840, the Nevada, without assistance, cleared berth F-8 and proceeded down
the South Channel where ... considerable damage was received when in the
vicinity of Yard Floating Drydock No. 2. In spite of the punishment taken and
the fact that her bridge and fore-structure were ablaze, the ship was fought
effectively throughout. In the same vicinity, at about 0910, and while
attempting to negotiate the turn in the channel, Nevada temporarily grounded."
At 10:35 she got clear and at 10:45 "voluntarily grounded on Waipio Peninsula
opposite Hospital Point". Second-wave dive bombers from the south-west attacked
the channel area in Phase III (CINCPAC: "It is probable that this group did
considerable damage to the U.S.S. Nevada").

### 4.5 Candidate moments

**P1. Welch or Taylor's first sortie (US, P-40B).** *Recommended.* Start about
08:35, 3 minutes; north of ʻEwa field, heading south, closing a line of

abandoned their whole fighter defense system when they encountered no initial

pilot; out of ammunition. After-text from §4.3; the Japanese count of 29 for

**P2. Taylor's second take-off (US, P-40B).** Start about 08:55 at Wheeler, on

**P3. Nevada's sortie (US, helm and AA).** *Recommended as the battleship

## 10. Decisions for the designer

**D1. Which clock the battle box shows.** *Recommend:* the battle's local
clock (Midway zone +12; Pearl Harbor HST; Ten-Gō JST), with the other clocks
on the card, and `{clock, zone, sourceClock, sourceZone}` on every beat. Why:
the three Midway clocks are the commonest source of error, and most tellings
on both sides use Midway local.

**D2. How to draw the Kidō Butai at 10:20.** *Recommend:* a `schematic` box
built to the constraints in §3.2 (Kaga SW, Akagi N of Kaga, Sōryū E, Hiryū NE
and separating; destroyers close in; the heavy ships 10–20 km out), and a task
for a person to check it against Parshall and Tully and Lundstrom before v1
ships. Why: no reachable source gives the spacing, and the playable moments
M1–M3 depend on the relative positions.

**D3. VT-8.** *Recommend:* not a sortie; a stop with a card. Why: §3.11 M4.

**D4. Japanese roles at Midway.** *Recommend:* offer M5 (CAP against VT-3),
M7 (D3A on Yorktown) and M9 (B5N on Yorktown), subject to R8. Why: the brief
draws both navies with the same care; these are fleet-against-air fights
between combatants; each has a clean record and a sortie-terms score.

**D5. Scripted outcome against the player's result.** *Recommend:* the
record's hits always happen; a player's hit where the record has a miss is
scored and the card says "the record: a near miss here". Why: the brief keeps
the arc fixed; showing the player's result beside the record is the point of
the card.

**D6. Which telling the scripted beats follow.** *Recommend:* the ship's own
report for hits on that ship (Nagumo for the Japanese carriers, Yorktown and
CINCPAC for Yorktown), with the other tellings on the card. For Akagi, follow
Nagumo's three bombs but draw the third as bursting at the stern edge (it
satisfies both tellings visually); the card gives both. Why: the target knew
best where it was hit; the attackers' claims run two to three times too high.

**D7. Positions and scale.** *Recommend:* centre the Midway morning box on the
NWC D-3 attack plot (about 30°08′N 179°00′W) and the noon box on Yorktown's
D-3 position (about 30°52′N 176°50′W); start sorties 1–15 miles from the
target in true scale; no compression inside a sortie. Why: the strikes'
approach legs are long, but every moment above begins at its contact point.

**D8. Altitude and parameters marked *verify*.** *Recommend:* use the values
in §3.6 (all from the record) and treat doctrine numbers (SBD dive 70°,
B5N drop height, P-40 altitudes, Philippine Sea raid altitudes) as tunable
until a person reads Lundstrom, Y'Blood and the doctrine manuals. Why: no
reachable digital source gives them.

**D9. The outline battles in later versions.** *Recommend:* P1 and P3 (Pearl
Harbor), T2 and perhaps T1 (Ten-Gō, ending at 12:45), PS1 (Philippine Sea),
S1 and S2 (Santa Cruz). Not: VT-9's final run, Battleship Row at 07:55–08:00,
or any player role in a crash.

**D10. Casualty display.** *Recommend:* per-ship and per-battle ranges from
§3.10 with sources, the 1947 "1 800" shown as printed and footnoted. Why: the
Hawaiian standard; the per-ship figures disagree by up to 20 %.

## 11. Risks

1. **Clock errors.** Three clocks at Midway, a 19½-hour difference at Pearl
   Harbor, and an unknown US zone at the Philippine Sea and Santa Cruz. A beat
   copied without conversion is hours wrong.
2. **Misprints in primaries.** Yorktown at 33°5x′N (CINCPAC, Fletcher),
   167°24′W (Yorktown); Hiryū at 179-23.5E (Nagumo Table 3); Kaga's dead
   "1 800"; Kaga's Air Control Unit "fc x 21" (Table 1, evidently 2); Fletcher's
   torpedo attack at "1541". A check should flag positions more than a few
   miles from the NWC plots.
3. **Inflated claims.** Japanese CAP and AA claims run several times the US
   losses; US claims of hits on Japanese ships run two to three times the
   Japanese counts. Cards must not repeat claims as results.
4. **Schematic formation presented as fact.** The Kidō Butai box (D2).
5. **Myths in the tertiary layer.** Fuchida's "five minutes"; "torpedo planes
   pulled the Zeros down" as settled fact; the Santa Cruz "suicide" planes; a
   B-26 "attempting to crash" Akagi (the Akagi TROM traces this to Muri's
   strafing pass).
6. **Tone.** Japanese roles shoot down slow torpedo planes; the Yamato helm
   role ends in her sinking; the P-40 second sortie begins under attack. R8
   should rule on each; this file recommends against VT-8, VT-9 and the first
   minutes on Battleship Row.
7. **Copyright.** TROMs and *Shattered Sword* are copyrighted; NWC diagrams,
   the Nagumo translation and US reports are public domain and may be traced.
   Japanese originals in JACAR may be cited, not reproduced.
8. **Positions read off diagrams.** ±5′ by eye from 1948 plots that are
   themselves reconstructions.
